### Commit 1: the marker and the writer
1. `MarkdownPageMarker`, exactly as the interface note's section 1 says. Normalizing: trim with `.whitespacesAndNewlines`; make each inner run of characters whose scalars are all in `.whitespacesAndNewlines` one space; cut to `maximumLabelLength` Characters (`prefix`); trim again. Escaping walks the label's Unicode scalars, keeping the previous scalar to spot a `-` after a `-`; a scalar to escape becomes `%` and two uppercase hexadecimal digits per UTF-8 byte (U+0000 is `%00`, U+2028 `%E2%80%A8`). `decode` implements its own percent-decoding over the UTF-8 bytes of the text between prefix and suffix (`%` plus two hexadecimal digits of either case is that byte; anything else is copied), then `String(bytes:encoding: .utf8)`, nil on invalid UTF-8; then the label goes through the same normalizing as `init` (share one private static function), and an empty result is nil. Comment why each escape exists: CommonMark (and swift-markdown's cmark-gfm) ends or refuses a comment at `--`, `>` ends the comment early, `<` and controls could open other markup or break the line.

### Commit 2: the page model and the parse
1. `TextMouth/Models/BookPages.swift`: `PageBreak`, `BookPages`, and `PagePosition`, exactly as the interface note's section 2 says, with one more function on `PageBreak`:
   ```swift
   /// Where a page that a marker places at `offset` (UTF-16, clamped to `text`) begins: the start of its first word (PG01).
   static func firstWordStart(in text: String, at offset: Int) -> Int
   ```
   At whitespace (`Character.isWhitespace` of the Character at the offset), the place moves forward past whitespace to the next Character that is not whitespace, or to the text's end; inside a word (the Character before the offset is not whitespace either), back to the word's start, with `ReaderViewModel.snappedWordStart(in:at:)`; at a word's first Character, or at the text's end, it stays. Comment why: an EPUB page-break element often stands just before the space that begins its page ("end.<span/> The next"), and a page can begin inside a hyphenated word; either way the page begins, for a listener, at its first whole word, so the place Next Page lands on and the page shown agree.
   - `BookPages.init` sorts by (chapterIndex, characterOffset, position in the given array), as `HeadingSweep.init` sorts, and computes `total` once. `wholeNumber(_:)`: nil unless the label is non-empty and every Character is an ASCII digit, then `Int(label)` (nil when too large). `index(ofPageAtChapter:offset:)` is a binary search for the first break that does not start at or before the place, minus one.
   - `PagePosition` gets an `init(pages: BookPages, index: Int?)` that `position(atChapter:offset:)` uses; `label` is exactly "Page \(label) of \(total)", "Before page \(firstLabel)", or "".
2. `Chapter` (DocumentContent.swift): `var pages: [PageBreak]`, documented ("The pages that begin in this chapter, in reading order, as the parse found their markers (PG01).
