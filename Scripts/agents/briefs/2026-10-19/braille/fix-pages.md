## L8: a blank row at a page's top ends a paragraph (`BraillePageAnalyzer.swift`)
A form feed followed by CR LF, or a form feed on a line of its own, starts the next page with a blank row, which ends a paragraph running over the page. BANA never starts a page with a blank line, so blank rows at a page's top (before its first row with text, after the numbers are taken out) are dropped. Test: the sample's two pages with `\f\r\n` between them, and with a form feed on a line of its own, give the same 14 blocks.

## M2: long cell-5 and cell-7 headings read as paragraphs (`BraillePageAnalyzer.swift`, about line 323, and `BrailleLayout.swift`)
At 40 cells, a cell-5 heading of 31 to 33 cells or a cell-7 heading of 27 to 29 measures centered (its right margin is within a cell of its indent) and becomes a paragraph, and a wrapped one a paragraph per row. Read back through the analyzer, the layout is misread in 375 of 1,000 of the fuzz's random books, and in 49 once that condition is removed.
1. **The analyzer classifies by the indent first:** a row of indent 4 or 6 that may begin a heading (after a blank row, the top of a page, the start of the book, or another heading) is a level-2 or level-3 heading whatever its right margin; only a row of any other indent can be a centered heading. Say in the comment that a centered heading whose left margin is 4 or 6 cells is therefore read as level 2 or 3: still a heading, its text whole.
2. **The layout never writes that ambiguous form:** a centered heading (levels 1 and 2) is wrapped at `cellsPerLine - 6` cells as now, but when any of its lines would then have a left margin of 4 or 6 cells, the whole heading is wrapped again at `cellsPerLine - 14` (a margin of at least 7). Comment why.
3. **The fuzz:** rebuilt from your branch, it gives 0 misreads of the layout's own output, or you name each remaining kind of misread in your report with one example (lists in cells 1-3 and 3-5 are known to merge into paragraphs; say how many of the misreads are that).
4. **Pin the boundary lengths** (`BraillePageAnalyzerTests`, `BrailleLayoutTests`): at 40 cells, cell-5 headings of 30 to 34 cells and cell-7 headings of 26 to 30 read as level 2 and 3; a wrapped cell-5 heading of two rows is one heading; centered headings whose lines would be 31, 32, 27, or 28 cells are wrapped at 26 and read back as level 1; the samples still lay out and read back exactly as before.

## M5: memory grows far past the file's size (`BrailleFileReader.swift` lines 116 to 145, `BraillePageAnalyzer.swift` lines 72 to 86)
Both run in TextMouth's own process and keep several copies of every row, about 370 bytes per page and 170 per row: tiny.brf peaked at 388 MB for 2 MB and 774 MB for 4 MB, and a realistic book takes about 22 times its size; at the 64 MB limit that is about 12 GB on this 8 GB Mac.
1. **A compact form:** the reader gives the analyzer every cell as one byte (its dots, 0 to 255) in one buffer, with the rows' and pages' ends as `Int32` offsets, built page by page as the file is read; the raw bytes (or the PEF's text) are not kept once read. Keep `pages: [[String]]` only as a computed property for the tests, or change the tests to the new form. The analyzer works on the compact form, and makes a `String` only for each finished block.
2. **Limits for files no book could be:** refuse a braille file of more than 100,000 pages or 4,000,000 rows (a 64 MB book of real braille is about 65,000 pages and 1,700,000 rows), each refusal a `BrailleFileError` with exactly: `This file has more than 100,000 braille pages, the most TextMouth reads.` and `This file has more than 4,000,000 lines of braille, the most TextMouth reads.`
3. **Measure** with the harness (`/usr/bin/time -l`, its maximum resident set) on big2 and big4's book, tiny, and rows files, before and after: the peak must be at most 4 times the file's size plus 30 MB for each. Report every figure.
4. The page entries (`BraillePageNumbers`) stay one per page; if they dominate the peak on tiny.brf, say so with the numbers rather than change their shape.
