## New formats and export (researched October 17)

Researched by team formats on October 17, 2026, in FORMATS-RESEARCH-2026-10-17.md, which gives each item's evidence and sources, and ends with 21 questions (numbered there) whose recommended answers these items assume. The user asked for more format support, "at least we could convert a bunch more audio formats to a standardized format like how we convert the text files to markdown", export of "any book (text or audio) to any other support format, giving the option to export part of a text book as audio too", and, for text, "rtf, dot, .doc, and braille .brf / .brl files (writing the encoder and a decoder for this as well)"; items marked "The user's wish" are those. The items run from the easiest to the hardest, an item that needs another coming after it. Difficulty: S is up to about 150 lines of Swift besides tests; M about 150 to 500, or a new path through pieces that exist; L about 500 to 1,000, a new library or helper, or a new playback path; XL more, or an undocumented format. Impact is for this user, 1 to 5. Sample files are in `TestData/format-samples/<folder>/`, listed in `samples.tsv` with each file's source, licence, and SHA-256; every audio sample is tones, never system-voice speech.

- [ ] **FMT01 — Word templates and macro documents (.dotx, .docm, .dotm).** Difficulty S · Impact 2 · The newer forms of the user's .dot. They are the same Office Open XML package as .docx with another main content type, and TextMouth's own Word reader opens fixed paths (word/document.xml, styles, numbering, relationships, core properties) and never the content type, so it reads all three as they are, with real headings; it reads only XML, so a macro project is never touched and no macro runs. Add the extensions wherever .docx is listed (`BookFormat` and the app's document types), with tests that each opens with its headings. About 5 lines plus tests; no size; no new risk. Samples: dotx/, docm/, dotm/ (a real LibreOffice file and a synthetic one each).
- [ ] **FMT02 — Say how to convert what TextMouth refuses.** Difficulty S · Impact 2 · The Nothing Imported alert already says what an archive holds ("It holds 176 .wma files, which TextMouth does not import."). Add a sentence of advice for the formats the research recommends refusing: Pages ("export it from Pages as Word or EPUB"), Keynote (to PowerPoint or PDF), Numbers (to Excel or CSV), WordPerfect and CHM (to Word or HTML), DjVu and XPS (to PDF), a real RAR comic (repack it as CBZ), and copy-protected audio (Audible .aa and .aax, .m4p: "TextMouth cannot open copy-protected files."). One table of extensions and sentences, used for a single file too, its wording pinned by tests; about 60 lines. Samples: pages/ (Pages 4 and 5 files), wpd/WP6.wpd.
- [ ] **FMT03 — Safari web archives (.webarchive).** Difficulty S · Impact 2 · A web archive is a binary property list whose WebMainResource holds the page's HTML and its text encoding. Read it with `PropertyListSerialization` (no WebKit, nothing loaded from the network), decode WebResourceData by WebResourceTextEncodingName, and give it to `HTMLToMarkdownConverter`, so headings, lists, and tables arrive as in an HTML book. About 40 lines plus tests. Risks: Safari's archives also hold subframes and many subresources (read only the main resource; cap the size); one written by Safari was not tried. Sample: webarchive/headings.webarchive.
- [ ] **FMT04 — A DTBook file on its own (.xml), and NIMAS.** Difficulty S · Impact 2 · The DAISY reader already reads DTBook in a DAISY 3 package, and in a ZIP or folder without an OPF, so a NIMAS package (DTBook, OPF, images) should import today (untested: add a test); a single loose .xml file fails, since `BookFormat` does not list it. Sniff an .xml file's root element for dtbook, refuse other XML by name, and give the reader a one-file provider: about 55 lines plus tests. NIMAS files reach students through schools and Bookshare, never as public files. Samples: dtbook/harbour-standalone.xml, dtbook/nimas-style-package/.
- [ ] **FMT05 — Nero chapter lists (chpl) in M4B and M4A files.** Difficulty S · Impact 3 · AVFoundation reads an MPEG-4 file's QuickTime chapter track, MP3's ID3 CHAP frames, and FLAC's CHAPTER comments, but not a Nero chpl atom alone (verified with a file whose chapter-track reference was renamed), and some audiobook tools write only chpl (inferred), so such a book shows no headings. When AVFoundation finds none, read the atom by hand (a count, then each chapter's start in 100-nanosecond units and its title), bounds-checked; about 80 lines plus tests. Sample: m4b-chapters/three-tones.m4b holds both a chapter track and chpl; a test can make the chpl-only form by renaming the track reference, as the research did.
- [ ] **FMT06 — Audacity labels, in and out.** Difficulty S · Impact 2 · Audacity's label file is UTF-8 text, a label a line: start, end, and text, tab-separated, in seconds; equal times make a point, others a region, and a line starting with a backslash holds a spectral selection, to skip. Import a label file beside an audiobook as bookmarks (as headings once FMT24 brings headings inside one file), and export a book's headings, bookmarks, or the audio editor's markers as labels, for a round trip with an audio editor. About 60 lines for the format, plus the commands and their wording. Samples: audacity-labels/ (labels.txt and labels-spectral.txt beside a four-second tone).
- [ ] **FMT07 — SSML files, imported as text.** Difficulty S · Impact 2 · An .ssml file becomes a text book with its markup removed (paragraphs and sentences kept), never handed to the speech engine as SSML or as text with tags: `AVSpeechUtterance(ssmlRepresentation:)` (macOS 13 and later) turns off the book's rate and pitch, ignored sub aliases, and crashed the research probe on every emphasis element (macOS 26.3.1), and `SpeechMarkupGuard` exists because the engine mistakes tag-shaped text for markup. About 100 lines plus tests; native SSML speech is set aside. Sample: ssml/passage.ssml (it holds the emphasis that crashed the probe, on purpose).
- [ ] **FMT08 — Pronunciation lexicons (PLS) as substitution rules.** Difficulty S · Impact 2 · A W3C PLS 1.0 lexicon's alias entries map onto TextMouth's substitution rules (grapheme to pattern, alias to replacement, whole word, case-insensitive), imported into a book's or the global rules and exported from them. Its IPA phonemes have no text form (Apple takes no lexicon file, and IPA works only per word through `AVSpeechSynthesisIPANotationAttribute`), so phonemes are set aside. About 150 lines plus tests. Sample: pls-lexicon/lexicon.pls.
- [ ] **FMT09 — Audio formats macOS already decodes.** Difficulty S · Impact 3 · The user's wish ("convert a bunch more audio formats to a standardized format"). Verified on files made here: afconvert and AVFoundation read AC-3, E-AC-3, AMR-NB (.amr and in .3gp), MP1 and MP2, NeXT/Sun .au and .snd, AIFC (IMA4, μ-law), CAF (PCM, ALAC, Opus), Wave64, RF64, BWF, and 3GP with AAC, so only their extensions are missing (aifc, caf, au, snd, w64, ac3, ec3, eac3, mp1, mp2, mpa, amr, awb, 3gp, 3g2, 3ga). Generalize `OggAudioConverter` into one import converter whose plan says, per file, convert to AAC .m4a with the watched afconvert (every coded format, so Apple's rarer decoders stay out of the player, as October 14 taught) or play as it is (plain PCM), each checked by `AudioMediaProbe` as Ogg is; Ogg keeps its own header reader. About 150 lines plus tests; no size. Risks: hostile files in Apple's decoders, contained by the child process; AMR-WB untested. Samples: ac3/, eac3/, amr/, mp2/, au/, aifc/, caf/, w64/, rf64/, bwf/, 3gp/, wav-variants/.
- [ ] **FMT10 — M3U and M3U8 playlists.** Difficulty S · Impact 3 · A playlist of local files becomes one book of tracks, as a ZIP or folder does, through a manifest written in memory: a heading per entry, titled by #EXTINF or else the file name, in the playlist's order. Decode with `TextFileDecoder` (a legacy .m3u is Windows-1252 or Latin-1, an .m3u8 UTF-8); accept relative paths, backslashes, percent escapes, and file: URLs; refuse an HLS streaming playlist (any #EXT-X- tag, or only http lines) in a sentence, and never fetch a URL. TextMouth is not sandboxed, so a playlist could name any file: use only files inside the playlist's folder (question 11) and name the entries skipped. About 150 lines plus tests. Samples: m3u/ (Latin-1, CRLF, accents, a backslash path, edge cases), m3u8/ (UTF-8, plus HLS media and master playlists), each with three one-second tone tracks.
- [ ] **FMT11 — Chapter markers in WAV, AIFF, and CAF exports.** Difficulty S · Impact 2 · The audio editor's Export says these three formats leave chapters out, but Core Audio writes markers into all of them: `AudioFileSetProperty` with `kAudioFilePropertyMarkerList` succeeded on each, writing cue and adtl, MARK, and mark chunks with the names, and ffprobe read the WAV's back (verified). Write the chapters as markers once the audio is written, drop the warning, and check which players read them. About 80 lines plus tests. Sample: bwf/ (a WAV with two cue markers).
- [ ] **FMT12 — PLS and XSPF playlists.** Difficulty S · Impact 1 · After FMT10, the same import for PLS (an INI file: FileN, TitleN, LengthN; mostly internet-radio links, which are refused) and XSPF (XML: trackList, location URIs resolved against xml:base, title, creator), with FMT10's folder rule. About 130 lines plus tests. Samples: pls-playlist/, xspf/.
- [ ] **FMT13 — RTF, Word 97-2003 .doc, its template .dot, RTFD, ODT, OTT, and WordML.** Difficulty M · Impact 5 · The user's wish ("rtf, dot, .doc"). Apple's `/usr/bin/textutil` reads all of them by content, a .dot exactly as a .doc (verified on files made here: 0.04 to 0.16 s and 12 to 15 MB for ordinary files, 1.1 s and 59 MB for a 3.3 MB RTF). Run `textutil -convert html` as a child process under `WatchedProcess`, as afconvert runs for Ogg, so a hang or crash in Apple's readers costs a child and not the import, and give its HTML to `HTMLToMarkdownConverter`: lists, tables, accents, and curly quotes survive. Headings do not: every format comes back with each heading as a bold, larger paragraph (verified, even RTF with Word's heading styles and ODT with real text:h), so add a pass that takes the body's size as the commonest by characters and makes a short bold or larger paragraph a heading, its level by size rank. An RTFD is a folder, imported as one book. About 220 lines with the runner, plus tests. Risks: bold run-in headings and decorative text fool the size rule (FMT47 is the fallback); encrypted and Word 6 and 95 files are untested. Samples: rtf/, rtfd/, doc/ (one made here and two real Word-format files from LibreOffice's tests with Heading 1 to 6), dot/ (a synthetic one with the template bit set, and a real one), odt/, ott/, wordml/.
- [ ] **FMT14 — Lossless audio stays lossless, and the original is kept.** Difficulty M · Impact 3 · The user's wish, kept honest. Convert lossless sources to Apple Lossless in .m4a (the same container, tags, and chapters as the AAC path; the editor already exports it) and lossy ones to AAC as today; this lets TextMouth accept Ogg FLAC, refused today, which afconvert turns into ALAC (verified). Keep the original beside the converted file in the package (the Ogg import deletes it, `FileImportService+Ogg.swift`), behind a setting (question 10), offered by Export's Original file. Correct docs/user/what-textmouth-reads.md, whose "so it stays about the same size and sounds no worse" a second lossy encoding cannot promise (ChatGPT's correction holds). About 200 lines plus tests. Risk: converted books take about twice the disk. Samples: caf/ (ALAC in CAF), flac-chapters/three-tones.flac, and the Ogg tones in TestData/audio.
- [ ] **FMT15 — CSV and TSV.** Difficulty M · Impact 3 · RFC 4180 is only informational: write a small state machine (quoted fields with commas, line breaks, and doubled quotes), sniff the delimiter (comma, semicolon, tab, pipe) from the first lines, decode with `TextFileDecoder`, and stream large files. A row becomes a paragraph, its cells joined by ", " and the header row first (today's HTML tables join cells with a space only, which reads badly), with a setting to say each column's name before its cell (question 15) and a row cap with a notice for huge files. About 250 lines plus tests. Samples: csv/books.csv, tsv/books.tsv.
- [ ] **FMT16 — Text files in other encodings.** Difficulty M · Impact 4 · `TextFileDecoder` reads anything that is not UTF-8 or UTF-16 as Windows-1252, so Mac Roman, Central European, Cyrillic (Windows-1251, KOI8-R), and Japanese or Chinese text files arrive garbled, with no word to the listener. Foundation's `NSString.stringEncoding(for:encodingOptions:convertedString:usedLossyConversion:)` named the right encoding for 11 of 13 short files made here, but not for Mac Roman or KOI8-R, and it never says it is unsure. Use it as a guess after today's checks, name the encoding in Book Info ("Read as Windows-1251"), and add a Reopen with Encoding command (question 2); a letter-frequency check can catch Mac Roman and KOI8-R. About 150 lines plus the command's sheet and tests. Samples: txt-encodings/ (one short text in 13 encodings, each file named for its encoding).
- [ ] **FMT17 — Markers and Ogg chapter comments read as chapters.** Difficulty M · Impact 2 · After FMT05, the chapter sources TextMouth still ignores: a WAV's or BWF's cue and adtl labels and a CAF's mark chunk (afinfo lists them; AVFoundation's chapter call does not, inferred), read with Core Audio's marker list; and Ogg's CHAPTERnn comments, read by `OggHeaderReader` and written into the converted .m4a's chapter track, which the editor's `AudioChapterTrack` already writes. About 200 lines plus tests. Samples: bwf/ (two markers), caf/.
- [ ] **FMT18 — Export with conversion: a Convert to choice, with HTML first.** Difficulty M · Impact 4 · The user's wish ("exporting any book (text or audio) to any other support format"). The Export dialog only copies today. Give each kind a Convert to choice (text: HTML here, then FMT19, FMT22, FMT28, and FMT29; audio: FMT30), a job runner with the dialog's progress, Stop, and VoiceOver sentences, and names that never replace a file, as now. The first writer is HTML from content.md through TextMouth's own swift-markdown walker (escaped XHTML with h1 to h6 and ids, the language, a title, a contents list), because swift-markdown 0.9.0's `HTMLFormatter` inserts text unescaped, so an & or < breaks the page, and drops heading ids (verified in its source); the same walker feeds every text writer. About 300 lines plus tests.
- [ ] **FMT19 — RTF export with real outline levels, and .doc through Apple's writer.** Difficulty S · Impact 3 · The user's wish (RTF, .doc). After FMT18. Apple's RTF writer drops heading styles (verified), so write RTF from the walker (a font table, a style sheet with heading 1 to 6 and \outlinelevel, Unicode as \uN), about 120 lines, and check in Word that its navigation pane lists the headings. Offer .doc only through `NSAttributedString`'s .docFormat writer, saying its headings are plain: writing Word's binary format ourselves is unwise. Sample: rtf/word-styles.rtf is the form to write.
- [ ] **FMT20 — Reading BRF, BRL, and PEF files.** Difficulty M · Impact 3 · The user's wish (the decoder). BRF is the 64-character North American Braille ASCII (0x20 to 0x5F, lowercase read as uppercase) in finished pages, usually 40 cells by 25 lines with form feeds; BRL is the same characters unformatted (Orbit Reader's guide); PEF is XML of Unicode braille rows. Map each character to its Unicode cell (the fixed table, verified 64 of 64 against liblouis's own), split pages at form feeds or every 25 lines, take the braille page number from the last line, find headings from centered lines with blank lines around them and from cell-5 and cell-7 lines (BANA's Braille Formats, 2016), and turn uncontracted (Grade 1) UEB into print with TextMouth's own decoder (about 110 lines; the prototype round-trips the sample). Contracted braille needs FMT21. About 400 lines with the page model, plus tests. Risks: BRF has no standard (widths 18 to 80, CRLF or LF, 8-dot files), and headings are a heuristic. Samples: brf/ (Grade 1 Pride and Prejudice chapter 1, two pages, with its print text; a short Grade 2 passage with its contractions named), brl/, pef/.
- [ ] **FMT21 — Contracted braille through liblouis.** Difficulty M · Impact 5 · The user's wish (a decoder and an encoder that work on real BRF files). BARD's and Bookshare's BRF files are contracted UEB (Grade 2), and turning them into print, or print into Grade 2, needs a translator. liblouis is the standard (LGPL 2.1 or later, the UEB tables too): vendor its release in Vendor/liblouis and build it statically as LAME is, with its source, licence, and notice, bundling only the UEB tables (9 files, 239 KB on master; about 0.5 to 0.7 MB with the library), and call it on one serial queue (treat it as not thread-safe). First, a one-day spike: macOS 26 ships liblouis 3.34.0 privately and adds a public `AXBrailleTranslator` (Accessibility.framework), which gave a command-line probe no tables (verified); if an app bundle gets the UEB tables, use it on macOS 26 and liblouis on 14 and 15 (question 5). About 200 lines of Swift besides the library, plus tests. Risk: back-translation is ambiguous (no accuracy figure was found), and a wrong contraction misreads a book. Samples: brf/grade2-short.brf with its print text and contractions.
- [ ] **FMT22 — Export to BRF and PEF.** Difficulty M · Impact 4 · The user's wish (the encoder). After FMT18, and FMT21 for Grade 2. Lay out pages as BANA's Braille Formats (2016) says: 40 cells by 25 lines by default, centered headings with blank lines around them, paragraphs in cells 3 and 1, no word divided at a line's end, the braille page number at the right of the last line, a form feed between pages, CR LF; BRF in braille ASCII, PEF as XML. Settings: cells per line (18 to 80), lines per page, grade 1 or 2, page numbers, and line ends (question 7). Grade 1 can be TextMouth's own (the research prototype encodes, lays out, and writes both formats, with a reader and decoder, in 428 lines of Python); Grade 2 is liblouis. About 250 lines with liblouis, 500 to 600 with our own Grade 1, plus tests. Samples: brf/ and pef/ are the prototype's output, passing its 57 checks.
- [ ] **FMT23 — SRT and WebVTT transcripts.** Difficulty M · Impact 4 · Attach an existing transcript to an audiobook instead of transcribing it, on any macOS: a .srt or .vtt beside a track with the same name (lecture.vtt beside lecture.m4a, or per track in a ZIP or folder), or chosen by a Book menu command (question 12). Each cue becomes a `TranscriptSentence` (text, start, end, no word times), and WebVTT's voice tags become "Name: " text, with the file's fingerprint and length recorded as the recognizer records them. Export a transcript as either format, a file per track or one for the book. One cue model, two readers, two writers: about 250 lines plus tests. Whether a saved transcript shows on macOS 14 and 15 is assumed, not checked. Samples: srt/, vtt/ (voice tags, NOTE, both time forms), each beside a four-second tone.
- [ ] **FMT24 — CUE sheets.** Difficulty M · Impact 4 · A heading per track for one-file audiobooks and CD rips: FILE, TRACK, TITLE, PERFORMER, and INDEX 01 in minutes, seconds, and frames of 1/75 s (INDEX 00 pregaps ignored); several FILEs map onto the manifest's segments; the sheet is decoded by `TextFileDecoder`; a FILE that does not match falls back to the same stem beside the sheet (sheets often name a .wav later compressed to .flac). Also the FLAC CUESHEET block, which AVFoundation does not read (verified), an APE or WavPack Cuesheet tag (with FMT31), and CUE export of a book's chapters. Paths outside the sheet's folder follow question 11. About 350 lines plus tests. Samples: cue/ (album.cue with a BOM, CRLF, a pregap, and frame offsets; two-files.cue in Latin-1 over two files), flac-chapters/three-tones.flac (a CUESHEET block).
- [ ] **FMT25 — A podcast episode's own transcript and chapters.** Difficulty M · Impact 5 · Podcasting 2.0 feeds link a transcript (WebVTT, SRT, JSON, HTML, or text) and a chapters file (JSON, version 1.2.0); TextMouth stores both links on each item (`FeedItem.transcripts` and `.chapters`) and never fetches them. After FMT23, fetch them beside the episode's download under its network rules (WebVTT first, then JSON, then SRT; text and HTML carry no times; question 13), make the transcript the episode's (text at once, on any macOS, with no recognition), and make the chapters its headings when it has none of its own (startTime and title; toc false hidden). About 300 lines plus tests. Risks: third-party text (cap its size, treat it as data, follow none of its links), and a transcript of another edition, since dynamic ads shift times (compare durations). Samples: podcast-transcript-json/, podcast-chapters-json/.

- [ ] **FMT31 — Audio formats macOS cannot read, through the installed ffmpeg.** Difficulty M · Impact 3 · The user's wish. macOS refuses WMA, WavPack, TTA, Speex, all Matroska and WebM, FLV, and AVI with MP3 (verified), and has no APE or Musepack decoder. `FFmpeg.swift` already finds an installed ffmpeg, and Homebrew's has aac_at, Apple's own AAC encoder inside ffmpeg (verified), so one watched run converts and keeps chapters and tags: `-nostdin -protocol_whitelist file`, the demuxer forced from TextMouth's own sniff of the first bytes, `-map 0:a:N -map_chapters 0 -map_metadata 0 -c:a aac_at` at the rate the Ogg converter would choose, or ALAC for lossless sources (FMT14), under `WatchedProcess`'s limits. Samples: wma/, wv/, tta/, spx/; APE, Musepack, DSD, and WMA Pro, Lossless, and Voice are listed by URL only in the research document.

- [ ] **FMT33 — PowerPoint slides (.pptx, .ppsx).** Difficulty M · Impact 3 · Lecture slides as a book: each slide's title a heading (else "Slide N"), its text in order, tables a row at a time, pictures' alt text, and optional speaker notes; slide order from presentation.xml's sldIdLst through its relationships, not file names; hidden slides skipped.
