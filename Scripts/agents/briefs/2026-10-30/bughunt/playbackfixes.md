# bughunt-playbackfixes: the end of a chaptered audiobook, bookmark order, the sleep timer and a chapter download after a stop, a spoken range past its text, and a waiting play that holds the main thread

Worker slug `bughunt-playbackfixes`, branch `bughunt/playbackfixes` (from `team/bughunt`). Read `README.md` beside this file first. Six small fixes, one commit each, each with its test. All six were verified by the lead by reading (5 and 6 come from the orchestrator's triage of the TODO).

## 1. A one-file audiobook with chapter markers stops before its audio ends
`ReaderViewModel+Playback.swift:2150-2156` sets a clip end for every chapter of a `.chaptersInOneFile` book, the last one included: `chapterStartTime(currentChapterIndex) + chapterChars / audioCharsPerSecond`. Chapter lengths are placeholder text, 10 characters a second (`AudioBookParser.placeholderLength(for:)`, `AudioBookParser.swift:8-10`, `ReaderViewModel.audioCharsPerSecond`), truncated to whole tenths, and `AudioLayout.chapterStartTime(chapterIndex:chapterUTF16Lengths:)` (`:1858-1870`) sums those truncated lengths. So the last chapter's clip end falls up to 0.1 s per chapter before the file's real end (about 1.4 s at 30 chapters, 5 s at 100); the clip end fires `.finishClip` (`AudioPlaybackService.swift:218`), the book is announced finished and counted in Books Finished, and its last seconds never play. Between chapters nothing is lost: each chapter's end and the next one's start are the same drifted time.

Fix: the last chapter of a one-file chaptered book gets no clip end, so the file's own end finishes the book. Put the decision in one function on `AudioLayout` beside `chapterStartTime` (for example `clipEnd(ofChapter:chapterUTF16Lengths:) -> TimeInterval?`: the chapter's end for a chaptered file's chapters but the last, nil for the last and for every other layout), with a doc comment saying why, and have the switch at `:2150` use it. Before you commit, read how the reader handles the file's natural end for a chaptered book (the audio service's finish report and `ReaderViewModel+Playback.swift`'s playback-finished path) and confirm that it ends the book as the last clip end did (the same end-of-book handling, `handleEndOfBook()`); if it does not, stop and ask the lead.

Test, in `AudioLayoutTests`: for `.chaptersInOneFile` with lengths [20, 30, 45], chapters 0 and 1 end at 2.0 s and 5.0 s, and chapter 2 has no clip end; every other layout has none. If `EndBehaviorMatrixTests` (grep its chaptered column) or `FinishedBookReaderTests` can play a two-chapter chaptered file through to its end silently (grep how they build one), add that the book ends at its file's end, not before.

## 2. Next and Previous Bookmark go by the bookmarks' numbers, not their places
`ReaderViewModel+Bookmarks.swift:170-197`: `goToNextBookmark()` sorts by `number` and takes the first after the cursor; `goToPreviousBookmark()` sorts by `number` descending and takes the first before it. Numbers follow the order bookmarks were made (`Library.swift:518`, `:534-536`), so with bookmark 1 in chapter 10 and bookmark 2 in chapter 3, Next from chapter 1 jumps to chapter 10, passing bookmark 2, and Previous from chapter 12 lands in chapter 3, passing bookmark 1.

Fix: both order bookmarks by their place in the book, `(chapterIndex, characterOffset)`, ties by `number`; Next takes the first after the cursor, Previous the last before it. Each still announces its own number ("Bookmark 2"), unchanged. One shared ordering, not two sorts written twice.

Test, in `PlaybackLifecyclePolicyTests` (grep how it sets bookmarks and the cursor): bookmark 1 at offset 700 and bookmark 2 at offset 100 of one chapter, and bookmark 3 in a later chapter; Next from 0 goes to 100 ("Bookmark 2"), then 700 ("Bookmark 1"), then bookmark 3; Previous from the end goes back through them in the reverse order. The announcement each time is pinned.

## 3. A stop that is not a pause leaves a minutes sleep timer counting down while nothing plays
`finishStopping(wasActive:wasSleepTimerFading:)` (`ReaderViewModel+Playback.swift:522-549`), which every `stop()` reaches, halts the sleep timer only when it was fading; a minutes timer's countdown runs on. It is reached by a cloud chapter's failed download (`ReaderViewModel+CloudDownload.swift:566`, `:835`), the Ogg refusal (`ReaderViewModel+OggConversion.swift:53`), Release Media Controls (`ReaderViewModel+Playback.swift:352`), and Edit Text replacing a playing book's text (`ReaderViewModel+EditedText.swift:102`). So a 30-minute timer keeps counting while nothing plays; the listener who plays again 12 minutes later has 18 minutes, or hears "Sleep timer expired. Playback paused." with nothing playing. Every pause already halts it until Play (`settleStopMadeBySpeech`, `:978-980`; the failure path, `:2339-2340`).

Fix: `finishStopping` calls `sleepTimerService.pauseCountdown(by: readerID)` when the timer was not fading (the service ignores a caller that does not own the timer and a timer that is not a minutes one, `SleepTimerService.swift:381-405`). The calls that then repeat it right after a stop become redundant: remove the one after `finishStopping` in the audio failure path (`:2339-2340`) and the one after `stop()` in `stopPlaybackForMemoryRunaway` (`:2386-2387`), so each stop pauses the countdown once (DRY). Closing a reader still stops a timer it owns afterwards (`ReaderViewModel.swift:2140-2142`), so nothing is left paused for a closed window.

Test, in `MediaCommandPlaybackTests`, beside `testARefusedTextHaltsTheSleepTimersCountdownUntilPlay` (copy its setup): play, start a 15-minute timer, `releaseMediaControls()`; `COUNTDOWN_PAUSED` is logged and the time left holds; Play logs `COUNTDOWN_RESUMED`. Also run the classes that pin the failure and memory paths' countdown (grep `COUNTDOWN_PAUSED` in the tests).

## 4. A spoken range past the end of its utterance ends TextMouth
`SpeechService.placeSpokenWord(at:of:)` (`SpeechService.swift:2451-2474`) takes the synthesizer's reported range straight into `NSString.substring(with:)` (`:2467`), which raises an Objective-C exception, ending the app, when the range runs past the utterance's text. Apple's synthesizer reports ranges by its own accounting (SSML utterances and voices have reported odd ranges before), so the app must not trust them.

Fix: when the range does not lie within the utterance's `speechString` (`NSMaxRange(range) <= length`, location not `NSNotFound`), ignore the report: no cursor move, one trace line in the log naming the range and the length, nothing announced. One small function deciding whether a reported range is usable, tested directly.

Test, in `SpeechDirectChunkingTests` or the class that already drives `placeSpokenWord` through a stand-in synthesizer report (grep `onWordSpoken`): a report of a range past the end leaves the cursor where it was and does not trap; a range inside moves it as today. Per the README, write the past-the-end report's test so it cannot trap even without the fix, by testing the decision function on its own; the test through the speech service runs only with the fix in place.

## 5. Stop, and so Release Media Controls, leaves a chapter's download waiting, and Play then does nothing (TODO-2026-09-04.md line 3947, from the orchestrator's triage)
`stop()` (`ReaderViewModel+Playback.swift:468-504`) lets go of a chapter's pause (`stopWaitingOutChapterPause`) and of audio set aside for a move (`letGoOfAudioSetAsideForMove`), but not of a chapter whose files it waits to download (FR02, a cloud library). `isWaitingForChapterDownload` (`ReaderViewModel+CloudDownload.swift:576-578`) keeps `isPlaybackUnderWay` true (`:3338-3343`), so `playOrResume()` ignores Play (`:176-184`) until the download ends, and then nothing plays. `pause()` already lets go of all three through `stopAudioWaitingToPlay(because:)` (`:3309-3313`).

Fix: `stop()` calls `stopAudioWaitingToPlay(because: "playback stopped")` in place of its two calls (DRY), and counts a chapter download in its `wasActive`, as it counts the chapter pause and the move. Item 3's countdown pause then applies to it too.

Test, in `ReaderCloudDownloadTests` (grep how it makes a reader wait for a chapter's download): Stop during a chapter's download, and Release Media Controls during one, each leave `isWaitingForChapterDownload` and `isPlaybackUnderWay` false and no download view; a Play afterwards starts the chapter (or waits for its download afresh, as Play from stopped does).

## 6. A settings change while Play waits for a long chapter's speech prepares that chapter on the main thread (TODO line 3867, from the triage)
`continueWaitingPlay()` (`ReaderViewModel+Playback.swift:1617-1630`) searches with `searchForSpeechStart(preparingLongChapters: .nowUnlessUnderWay)` (`:1623`). When the speech it waited for was prepared from settings that changed meanwhile (a voice, a rate, a substitution rule), that speech was not kept, so the long chapter is prepared again at once, on the main actor (`.nowUnlessUnderWay`, `:1752-1756`), holding the main thread, and VoiceOver with it, for as long as a long chapter takes.

Fix: search with `.inBackground` (`:1757-1759`) there, so the play goes on waiting, showing Playing, while the chapter is prepared again off the main thread from the settings in force, and starts once it is ready (`.preparingInBackground` → `waitForChapterSpeech`). Short chapters are prepared as before (the option governs long chapters only; confirm that by reading `searchForSpeechStart`). Update the doc comment (it says "at once").

Test, in `SpeechPreparationCacheTests` (grep how it makes Play wait for a long chapter's speech and changes a setting meanwhile): a settings change during the wait prepares the chapter again off the main thread (the class's way of observing where preparation runs), and the play starts once, from the settings in force.
