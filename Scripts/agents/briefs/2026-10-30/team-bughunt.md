Read `teams-common.md` beside this file first: it is the pattern, the rules, and the report format, and it stands in for AGENTS.md. You are the lead, `bughunt-lead`, on branch `team/bughunt`, made from local `main` by name (`git checkout -b team/bughunt main`; your worktree may start on an older commit).

The areas below have had the least review lately; the newest features (pages, braille, encodings, transcripts, Replace Existing) had full reviews on October 21 to 25 and are not your first target. ## Phase A: five read-only reviewers, in parallel
Launch five reviewers at once with the Agent tool (`subagent_type: "general-purpose"`, `model: "opus"`, no worktree isolation needed since they change nothing; give each `description: "bughunt review <area>"`). Each prompt must say, in its first lines: it is a read-only reviewer of TextMouth (a native macOS text-to-speech and audiobook player for a blind VoiceOver and braille user) at `/Users/roberteggleston/programming/ai/claude/swift/TextMouth`, branch `main`; it changes no file, builds nothing, and runs no test; **it never reads, copies, or writes anything under `~/.TextMouth`, the user's live library**; it reads by line range and greps rather than whole files twice; and its final message is its report, also written to `/Users/roberteggleston/programming/ai/claude/swift/TextMouth/build/orchestrator/oct30/bughunt-review-<area>.md`.

The report lists at most 15 findings, ranked, each with: a one-line summary; the file and line; a concrete failure scenario (the input or sequence of events, and the wrong result: lost data, a crash, a hang, wrong words, a wrong state); how sure it is (confirmed by reading every step, or plausible); the fix in two or three sentences; and the test that would pin it. No style remarks, no "consider", nothing it has not traced through the code. It also says, in a line, what it checked and found sound, so the next hunt can skip it.

The five areas:
1. **`playback`:** the reader's playback lifecycle. `TextMouth/Services/SpeechService.swift`, `AudioEngineService.swift`, `AudioPlaybackService.swift`, `NowPlayingService.swift`, `MediaGestureDetector.swift`, `SleepTimerService.swift`, `PlaybackMemoryGuard.swift`, `ReadingStatsService.swift`, and `TextMouth/ViewModels/ReaderViewModel+Playback.swift`, `+GoingOn.swift`, `+Navigation.swift`, `+Jump.swift`, `+WhereAmI.swift`, `+SavedPlaces.swift`, `+Bookmarks.swift`, `ChapterSpeechCache.swift`. Look for: a position saved wrong or not at all (on pause, close, quit, sleep, a route change, the end of a chapter or book), stale callbacks from an old utterance or player acting on a new one, two sounds at once, a sleep timer that misfires, statistics counted twice or lost, and main-thread hangs.
2. **`feeds`:** podcasts and article feeds. `TextMouth/Services/Feeds/`, `TextMouth/Views/Feeds/`, `TextMouth/ViewModels/LibraryViewModel+Feeds.swift`, `+FeedDownloadReturn.swift`, `+FeedItemMove.swift`, `+DeleteFeed.swift`, `+FeedDownloadProgress.swift`, `ReaderViewModel+EpisodeDownload.swift`, `NetworkReachability.swift`. Look for: an episode's position, transcript, or download lost on refresh, re-download, unsubscribe, a moved or renamed feed folder, or a feed whose items change ids; downloads that never finish or run twice; a feed's XML (untrusted) that crashes or hangs the parser; wrong counts of new items.
3. **`library`:** the library's data safety. `TextMouth/Services/LibraryPersistenceService.swift`, `KeptBooks.swift`, `StorageGeneration.swift`, `LibrarySessionLease.swift`, `DataDirectoryMigrator.swift`, and `TextMouth/ViewModels/LibraryViewModel.swift`, `+BookOperations.swift`, `+FolderManagement.swift`, `+ReadingList.swift`, `+SortAndOrder.swift`, `+IndexRecovery.swift`, `+MoveToLibrary.swift`, `LibraryListCache.swift`. Look for: a write that can lose a book, a position, a bookmark, a folder, or the reading list (an interrupted save, two windows changing the same thing, a failure path that leaves memory and disk disagreeing); a list cache that shows a stale list after a change; folder renames and moves that leave a book, a sort, or a feed behind.
4. **`traps`:** crashes from input, across all production code under `TextMouth/` (not tests). Every Swift trap source reachable from a file, a feed, a network reply, `config.json`, `library.json`, or a package record: force unwraps and `try!`; subscripts and ranges built from parsed numbers (`a..<b` with `a > b` traps); `Int(_:)` or `UInt8(_:)` of a `Double` that may be NaN, infinite, or out of range, and integer arithmetic that can overflow on large values; division by zero; `String.Index` used on another string; `Array(repeating:count:)` with a negative count; `fatalError` and `precondition` reachable from input; unbounded recursion on nested input (XML, HTML, JSON, ZIP in ZIP); and `unowned` captures. Start with the parsers in `TextMouth/Parsers/` and the readers of headers (`OggHeaderReader.swift`, `Services/MP3/`, `AudioFileInfo.swift`), then the services.
5. **`import`:** the import pipeline. `TextMouth/Services/FileImportService.swift`, `+Packages.swift`, `+AudioConversion.swift`, `+Recording.swift`, `ArchiveExtraction.swift`, `ArchiveBudget.swift`, `ImportStaging.swift`, `ImportScratchSpace.swift`, `ImportAudioConverter.swift`, `OrphanedConversionSweep.swift`, `TextutilConverter.swift`, `WatchedProcess.swift`, `DuplicateReplacement.swift`, `TextMouth/ViewModels/LibraryViewModel+Import.swift`. Look for: an original file deleted when the import did not finish; a half-imported book left in the library or a package left on disk with no row; a ZIP path that escapes its folder; a child process left running; scratch space never freed; and wrong words in an import's report.

While they run, wait without polling (they notify you when done).

## Phase B: verify, then fix
1. **Verify every finding yourself** by reading the code it names, before any fix: drop false positives and say why in your report. A plausible finding you cannot confirm by reading becomes a failing test first, and is fixed only if the test fails.
2. **Fix** the confirmed findings with workers, as `teams-common.md` says, one build at a time: each fix with a regression test that fails without it, data loss and crashes first. Group small fixes in one area into one task; keep tasks in different files apart.
3. Anything too large or needing the user's decision goes in your report's follow-ups, with the reviewer's evidence.

## Files
