# Document goldens
<!-- id: document-goldens; keys: ; src: TextMouthProductionTests/DocumentGoldenTests.swift -->

Part of [TextMouth's documentation](../../README.md).

Document goldens: `DocumentGoldenTests` compares every document in TestData with `TestData/golden/<file name>.golden`, and names every difference in words. A golden records the file's format, title, author, chapter and word counts, the length and checksum of its reading text, the first and last 300 characters of that text, its headings with their levels, and, for a book with audio, its tracks. The test takes about 1 second.

## Recording new goldens
<!-- id: recording-new-goldens; keys: ; src: TextMouthProductionTests/DocumentGoldenTests.swift, Scripts/test/run-area.py, Scripts/agents/heavy_lock.py -->

`Scripts/test/update-goldens.sh` records new goldens after a deliberate change to what a parser, the text pipeline, or heading detection produces. It runs `DocumentGoldenTests` alone with `TEXTMOUTH_UPDATE_GOLDENS=1`, which writes the goldens into the source tree instead of comparing, and then lists the goldens that changed. Review the diff of `TestData/golden` and commit it with the change it records; a golden that looks wrong, such as a missing heading or a wrong title, is a bug to report, not a record to commit. The two large optional DAISY books are never recorded. The script builds, through `run-area.py`, and neither takes the machine-wide heavy-work lock (`Scripts/agents/heavy_lock.py`; see The heavy-work lock in [Building TextMouth](building.md)), which only the build train and the iOS type-checks take for themselves. So run it under the lock: import `heavy_lock.py` by path (`python3 -I` leaves a script's own folder out of `sys.path`) and run the script inside `with held(owner, wait_seconds):`, so that it does not build beside a train or another build.

## The PDF golden's macOS version
<!-- id: pdf-golden-macos-version; keys: ; src: -->

A PDF's golden notes the macOS version it was recorded on, and its test skips on a Mac of another major version, since PDFKit and Vision change what they read with macOS. So do the goldens of the format samples in the rtf, rtfd, doc, dot, odt, ott, and wordml folders (`macOSDependentFormatSampleFolders`), recorded on macOS 26.3; the other format samples are compared on every Mac. The list still names the rtf, rtfd, doc, dot, odt, ott, and wordml folders, though TextMouth's own readers now read nearly all of their samples.

## Format samples with goldens
<!-- id: format-samples-goldens; keys: ; src: TextMouthProductionTests/TextPipelineFixtures.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/DocumentGoldenTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/RTFTextutilComparisonTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/DOCXRealFileTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/WordDocumentReaderTests.swift -->

The format samples in `TestData/format-samples` that TextMouth reads as text have goldens too: 46 of the 91 samples of the rich text, RTFD, Word, Word 97-2003, Word template, OpenDocument, Word 2003 XML, web archive, SSML, and DTBook folders (and the EPUB and PDF page samples), each named by its path inside that folder with every `/` written `__`, as in `rtf__headings.rtf.golden`. `TextPipelineFixtures.formatSampleNames` lists them, and `DocumentGoldenTests` fails when a sample of those folders is missing from it. `TextPipelineFixtures.formatSamplesHeldBack` names any sample whose golden is neither recorded nor compared, and why: one whose golden showed a reader bug, until the bug is fixed, and the 12 real RTF files from LibreOffice (`rtf/libreoffice-*.rtf`), held back because `RTFTextutilComparisonTests` compares their reading with textutil's word for word and names every difference, and a golden of textutil's reading would pin what TextMouth's own reader replaces (textutil refuses three of them outright). Three more groups are held back or left to a comparison test. The 9 real DOCX files from LibreOffice (`docx/libreoffice-*.docx`) are held back because `DOCXRealFileTests` asks them, by name, what hidden text, notes, and text boxes read as, where a failure says what changed. The 9 Word 97-2003 files added with that reader's follow-ups (`libreoffice-tdf102334_hiddenText.doc`, `libreoffice-tdf106541_chapterNumbering.doc`, `libreoffice-tdf112535.doc`, `libreoffice-tdf73056_cellMargins.doc`, `poi-61911.doc`, `poi-Bug51944.doc`, `poi-lists.doc`, `poi-rasp.doc`, and `poi-textboxes.doc`) are held back because `WordDocumentReaderTests` pins what each reads as and `WordDocumentComparisonTests` compares its words with textutil's. And the 15 real OpenDocument files from LibreOffice (`odt/libreoffice-*.odt`) have no goldens (`TextPipelineFixtures.isComparedByODTTests`, which is not part of `formatSamplesHeldBack`), since `ODTTextutilComparisonTests` compares their reading with textutil's and names every difference. The other real .doc and .dot files from LibreOffice are not held back: they have goldens, recorded again for the own reader. `Scripts/test/update-goldens.sh` records these goldens with the others. Only the Word 95 file `doc/libreoffice-tdf150197_anlv2ListFormat.doc` adds a textutil run; every other format sample is read by TextMouth's own readers and starts no textutil (the odt, ott, and wordml samples among them since 0.4.0).

## Comparison tests for rich text and Word
<!-- id: comparison-tests-rich-text-word; keys: ; src: TextMouthProductionTests/RTFTextutilComparisonTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/WordDocumentComparisonTests.swift, TextMouth/Services/Export/RTFExportWriter.swift, TextMouth/Services/NowPlayingService.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/WordDocumentLegacyComparisonTests.swift -->

`RTFTextutilComparisonTests` and `WordDocumentComparisonTests` (Mac only) are the check that let the Mac read rich text and Word 97-2003 with TextMouth's own readers in place of textutil. Each reads a sample both ways, textutil with `OwnDocumentReaders.$forcesTextutil` bound to true and the own reader directly, turns both into reading text with the converter an import uses, and compares them. Every difference is named in a table in the test, with the reason a listener would accept it; any other difference fails the test and is listed in its message.

- `RTFTextutilComparisonTests` compares the RTF samples `headings`, `page-breaks`, `word-list-table`, and `word-styles` and the two RTFD samples by their words (runs of letters and digits, in order) and requires every heading of textutil's reading in the own one; compares pages (every page that textutil finds, the own reading finds before the same words, and it may find more); compares the output of `RTFExportWriter` the same way; and tests the routing and the test hook. Its `realFiles` table lists the 12 real files (the reader now also differs from textutil in the notes of 14 and the text boxes of 5 of the 525 LibreOffice RTF test files, which the comparison of 2026-11-06 counted: 363 of the 411 that both read have the same words, 16 differ in spacing only, and textutil refuses 114 that the reader reads), each with its words only textutil has, its words only the own reader has, its headings, its pages, and why: `libreoffice-tdf146489.rtf`, which now names no difference in its list number (the number of a list item in a table cell is in the Markdown, and no list's number is in the reading text) and gained the words of its footnote, and `libreoffice-tdf130817.rtf`, whose two endnotes textutil drops and the own reader reads; `libreoffice-fdo53556.rtf`, where textutil runs a shape's property names into the first word of each text box ("fFilled0fLine0ARL") and the own reading is the words alone; `libreoffice-tdf108123.rtf`, where the own reader reads the words of a text box that textutil drops and begins a page at a section break that textutil's plain text loses (2 pages to 1); and three files that textutil refuses and the own reader reads: `libreoffice-tdf128428_dntblnsbdb.rtf`, `libreoffice-tdf121623.rtf`, and `libreoffice-tdf95706.rtf` (a `{\rtf` with no version number).
- `WordDocumentComparisonTests` reads every sample of the `doc` and `dot` folders. One test requires that the samples the reader leaves to textutil (it answers nil) are exactly `leftToTextutil`, the Word 95 files `libreoffice-tdf150197_anlv2ListFormat.doc` and `poi-Bug51944.doc` (an identifier of 0xA699 and a version of 0x68, a layout of Word 95 or older; the test reads with `readsOldLayout: false`, so these two stay with textutil here, and `WordDocumentLegacyComparisonTests`, below, reads them with the old layout). The others compare the words in order, textutil's headings (all must be the reader's, at any level), and the number of pages. Its named differences: `wordsOnlyTextutilReads`, the header text that textutil adds at the end of a document and the reader leaves out (`libreoffice-tdf104239_chapterNumbering.doc`, `libreoffice-tdf139495_tinyHeader.doc`, `libreoffice-fdo77844.doc`); `instructionWordsOnlyTextutilReads`, the field instructions that textutil reads as words (TITLE, SAVEDATE, LASTSAVEDBY, PRINTDATE, and MERGEFORMAT in `libreoffice-tdf98284_softLockedFields.doc`); and `headingsOnlyTextutilFinds`, the headings of textutil's size guess that the reader does not make (the "Contents" of a table of contents in `libreoffice-tdf81705_outlineLevel.doc`, whose style says body text, the header text of the tiny-header file, the company line that `poi-rasp.doc` makes a heading only by its mark's piece modifiers, and the three bold lines of `poi-textboxes.doc`, whose whole body is one cell of a layout table, in which the reader never guesses a heading). Three more tables name differences: `wordsOnlyTextutilReads` gained the header and footer of `poi-61911.doc`; `wordsOnlyTextutilJoins` names the nine one-letter cells of IDENTICAL in `libreoffice-tdf73056_cellMargins.doc`, which textutil runs into one word and the reader keeps apart; and `instructionWordsOnlyTextutilReads` gained the SHAPE instruction that textutil reads twice in `poi-textboxes.doc`. `listNumbersOnlyReaderReads` holds the numbers the reader would write with `WordDocumentReader.speaksListNumbers` on, which is off: the comparison of the words form takes each off the front of its paragraph, and the samples read as textutil's words by default.

## Word 6 and Word 95 tests
<!-- id: word-6-word-95-tests; keys: ; src: TextMouthProductionTests/WordDocumentLegacyTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/WordDocumentLegacyFeatureTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/WordDocumentLegacyHostileTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/WordDocumentLegacyComparisonTests.swift, TextMouth/Services/NowPlayingService.swift -->

Word 6 and Word 95 (0.4.1): `WordDocumentLegacyTests` and `WordDocumentLegacyFeatureTests` read documents built in code in the order Word writes them (`WordLegacyTestSupport`), `WordDocumentLegacyHostileTests` gives the old layout hostile files (every FIB field at its extremes, truncations, byte flips, and a loop of styles), and `WordDocumentLegacyComparisonTests` (Mac only) reads the samples of `TestData/format-samples/doc6` and the two old-layout files of `doc` with the reader and with textutil, and compares their words in order (the code-page rows of its table are the files textutil reads in the wrong code page), their brackets, their headings (the reader's must include textutil's), and their pages (a continuous section break begins none in the reader). `WordDocumentSymbolTests` and `WordDocumentSymbolFontTests` pin the reading of a symbol by its font in both layouts, each WordPerfect code, the picture fonts, the clones of Symbol, and text typed in a symbol font. A real-file comparison of 312 old-layout and 340 Word 97 government documents, which the repository does not hold, was run once through the app's pipeline against textutil and against main; its main figures are in the Imports page of the architecture.

## Reader tests for rich text and Word
<!-- id: reader-tests-rich-text-word; keys: ; src: TextMouthProductionTests/RTFReaderTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/WordDocumentReaderTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/CompoundFileTestSupport.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/RTFHostileReaderTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/WordDocumentHostileTests.swift -->

`RTFReaderTests` (55 tests) and `WordDocumentReaderTests` (129) test the readers on the samples and on documents built in code (`WordDocumentTestSupport` writes a Word 97-2003 document from a description of its paragraphs, styles, and sections, on `CompoundFileBuilder`'s compound file) for the structures the samples lack. `RTFHostileReaderTests`, `WordDocumentHostileTests`, and `CompoundFileTests` give them hostile files, which must end with a document or a typed failure, never a crash, in well under a second. `WordDocumentListTests` (43) reads Word's list tables in both forms, the numbers silent and spoken, with hostile list inputs, and times the two hangs the review found (a style chain walked for every character, and every piece walked for every note).

## Where the real sample files come from
<!-- id: where-real-sample-files-come; keys: ; src: -->

The real files are from the LibreOffice core repository's test data, under MPL-2.0, and from Apache POI's test documents, under Apache-2.0 (the five `doc/poi-*.doc` files, the first POI files here). `TestData/format-samples/samples.tsv` lists each with its source, licence, size, SHA-256, and what it exercises: 12 RTF files, 9 DOCX files, 15 OpenDocument files, and the Word 97-2003 files (`libreoffice-*.doc` and `.dot`, of which `libreoffice-tdf150197_anlv2ListFormat.doc` is Word 6 or Word 95, and the five POI files, of which `poi-Bug51944.doc` is also a layout of Word 95 or older, and the 16 files of the `doc6` folder, from POI and LibreOffice, of Word 6 and Word 95), besides the files made on this Mac. Each downloaded file was fetched into a folder of its own and read by a script run with `python3 -I`.

## DOCX tests built in code
<!-- id: docx-tests-built-code; keys: ; src: TextMouthProductionTests/DOCXHiddenTextTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/DOCXNotesTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/DOCXTextBoxTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/DOCXEmphasisTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/DOCXRealFileTests.swift -->

The DOCX tests build packages in code (`DOCXTestPackages`) for the cases the samples lack. `DOCXHiddenTextTests` pins hidden text through a run, its character style, and the paragraph style by the toggle rule of ECMA-376 17.7.3, and the text hidden only on the web; `DOCXNotesTests` pins the notes parts (separators skipped, only the notes a read reference points to, a damaged notes part losing only its notes); `DOCXTextBoxTests` pins a text box written twice, read once and after its paragraph; `DOCXEmphasisTests` requires that no `*` or `~` is spoken, with a sweep of 2,128 paragraphs of runs in every emphasis; and `DOCXRealFileTests` reads the 9 real files by name. Against the repository's 1,472 Word files (the 9 above, the DOCX, DOCM, DOTX, and DOTM samples, and 1,447 LibreOffice test documents under 60 KB), seven refuse to open, as they did before, and none speaks a mark that is not a character of its text.

## Symbols of Word documents
<!-- id: symbols-word-documents; keys: ; src: TextMouthProductionTests/DOCXSymbolTests.swift -->

The symbols of Word documents are pinned by `DOCXSymbolTests` and `WordMLSymbolTests` (TextMouthProductionTests/DOCXSymbolTests.swift), which build their packages in code (`makeDOCXPackage` takes `otherParts:` for `word/fontTable.xml`).

- They cover a `w:sym` in the Symbol font, in a font of pictures, in a WordPerfect font, and in an ordinary font, and symbol text typed in each; the order of a run's font (its own, its character style's, its paragraph style's, the default paragraph style's, the defaults'); a damaged font table never losing the book; and the Word 2003 XML half (`w:h-ansi`, `w:docDefaults`, `w:charset`).
- Four were added from the November 8 check, which found that deleting a guard in the code left the first 18 tests green: `testAHostileSymbolCharacterNeverTraps` (a `w:char` with a sign, a space, no digits, five digits, or 100,000 digits), `testASymbolInANotesCustomMarkIsSkippedWithTheMark`, `testASymbolInADeletionOrAMoveAwayIsNotRead`, and `testStylesBasedOnEachOtherInALoopEnd`.
- The tests were proved by 34 deliberate breaks of a throwaway SwiftPM copy of the sources, never of the branch.

## Speech export tests
<!-- id: speech-export-tests; keys: ; src: TextMouthProductionTests/SpeechBufferSynthesizerTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/SpeechChapterRendererTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/SpeechExportDialogTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/SpeechExportEncoderTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/SpeechExportJobTests.swift -->

The speech export has its own area, `[speech-export]` in Scripts/test/areas.conf, with nine classes: `SpeechBufferSynthesizerTests`, `SpeechChapterRendererTests`, `SpeechExportDialogTests`, `SpeechExportEncoderTests`, `SpeechExportJobTests`, `SpeechExportOptionsTests`, `SpeechExportServiceTests`, `SpeechExportSupportTests`, and `SpeechExportTextTests`. Run it with `python3 Scripts/test/run-area.py speech-export`.

- `SpeechChapterRendererTests` has an opt-in test that writes a real voice into a file. It runs only with `TEXTMOUTH_REAL_SPEECH_EXPORT=1`, which the test runner receives as `TEST_RUNNER_TEXTMOUTH_REAL_SPEECH_EXPORT=1`, so it is skipped in an ordinary run.
- The synthesizer sits behind `SpeechBufferWriting`, so `SpeechBufferSynthesizerTests` scripts it: a refusal, the stall limits, Stop, and the Personal Voice check, with no voice speaking.
- The tests of the sheet that host it (`testTheSheetDrawsTheOptionsWhileCheckedAndDisablesThemWhileExporting`, `testTheSheetWithTheOptionsFits`) were never run against a deliberate break; only a train shows they pass. No test reads the MP3 line, "Reading the book's headings…", or the unreadable-text line from the drawn sheet.
- The tests were proved by deliberate breaks of a throwaway SwiftPM copy of the sources (62 are listed in the brief's mutations.md); the clean copy passed and every break was caught after the laziness test was fixed.
- A real voice (Samantha) was exported to an M4B, an M4A, and an MP3, each with three chapters, in the November 8 check.
- The shared helper `debugLogLines(writtenWhile:)` (ConfigTestSupport.swift) reads the bytes the debug log gained while an export ran, since a log line's time is to the second only and an older line could look the same.

## Rich text follow-up tests
<!-- id: rich-text-follow-up-tests; keys: ; src: TextMouthProductionTests/RTFTrackedChangesTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/RTFNotesTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/RTFTableListTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/OwnReaderCancellationTests.swift, Scripts/test/kf8-fuzz/Stubs.swift -->

`RTFTrackedChangesTests`, `RTFNotesTests`, and `RTFTableListTests` pin the rich text follow-ups (a deletion left out and `\plain` undoing it, notes after the body and only those whose reference is read, a list item's number in a table cell). `OwnReaderCancellationTests` pins the shared cancellation check, `ImportCancellation.throwIfCancelled()`, in the rich text, DOCX, OpenDocument, Word 2003 XML, and MOBI readers: it ends a read when an import is cancelled and does nothing outside an import.

## OpenDocument and Word 2003 XML readers
<!-- id: opendocument-word-2003-xml-readers; keys: ; src: TextMouthProductionTests/WordMLReaderTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/ODTReaderTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/ODTHostileReaderTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/OfficeXMLGuardTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/XMLInputGuardTests.swift -->

The OpenDocument and Word 2003 XML readers have `ODTReaderTests`, `WordMLReaderTests`, `ODTHostileReaderTests`, and `OfficeXMLGuardTests` (the shared XML base: escaping and name scopes are linear in their input, a document type is found in every encoding, a tag of more than 1,024 attributes is refused and one with a very long attribute is not). `XMLInputGuardTests` pins the guard (see Shared helpers, in [Architecture](../../ARCHITECTURE.md)) on tags of 1,024 and 1,025 attributes, hostile shapes refused within 10 ms (an attribute flood, a subset, an encoding spelled with an alias or with a newline around the =), the encodings allowed, and the sentences; the readers that ask it (the feed, OPML, SMIL, PEF, and ODT manifest reads) have a test of their own that the guard is asked. `DOCXHiddenTextTests` pins the hiding rule's levels (the 243 combinations of the five levels were also compared, once, with a table built from the clauses of MS-OI29500 by a separate script), `DOCXPictureAttributeTests` the strip of `o:gfxdata`, and `MarkdownPageMarkerTests` that 20,000 page markers written in one paragraph take linear time, by a limit checked as it goes. `make check-ios-plist` and `IOSInfoPlistTests` check the iOS plist (see Building TextMouth). `ODTTextutilComparisonTests` reads the 15 real files with the reader and with textutil, and requires the same words, textutil's headings, and at least textutil's pages, except for named differences: the note bodies and text boxes the reader reads and textutil drops, the footnote marks textutil runs into words, the hidden sections textutil reads, and, in three files, the first and last words of the counted differences are pinned. `WordMLReaderTests` has no real Word 2003 XML sample: the two samples were written by textutil, and the reader was written from the format and checked on a hand-made document that covers every rule. A real sample was not added because the one candidate is over the size preference and has no licence file.
