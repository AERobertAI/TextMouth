## The markup guard tests
<!-- id: markup-guard-tests; keys: ; src: TextMouth/Parsers/MarkupGuard.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/MarkupGuardTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/MarkupFuzzGenerator.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/ImportFuzzMutator.swift, TextMouth/Parsers/HTMLParser.swift -->

`MarkupGuardTests` (TextMouthProductionTests/MarkupGuardTests.swift, with `MarkupFuzzGenerator`) tests the guard that every SwiftSoup parse goes through (see Shared helpers, in [Architecture](../../ARCHITECTURE.md)). Its documents are made so that a failure is a failed assertion and not a trapped test host, which a trap would end:

- Every shape of broken declaration reads with the words around it, `testWhichDeclarationsTrap` holds the table of which trap SwiftSoup, and `testTheParserIsTheOneSwiftSoupWouldChoose` holds the choice of parser.
- `testTheDepthLimitIsExact` checks the guard's own count at the limit and one level past it, without converting more than 38 levels. The cases count what SwiftSoup's HTML parser builds, tag by tag, in `testThePassCountsWhatTheHTMLParserBuilds` and exactly for XML in `testThePassCountsWhatTheXMLParserBuildsExactly`, and `testRandomMarkupGoesNoDeeperThanThePassCounts` holds random documents from `MarkupFuzzGenerator` (SplitMix64, so a document depends only on its seed and round) to the count.
- The repeated-unit test, `testRepeatedUnitsAreCountedAsDeepAsTheParserBuildsThem`, takes 15 units of one to three tags that walked past the first count (`<frameset><table>`, `<marquee><table><tfoot>`, `<form><div></form>`, and the like) in three heads, 30 times over, and requires the count to be at least the real tree's depth. The harness holds every unit of one to three of 108 tags, in three contexts and in a body fragment (5,085,936 units), to the same at 40 and at 2,000 repeats.
- The second-layer test, `testTheParsedTreeIsHeldToTheLimitToo`, calls `MarkupGuard.requireDepth(of:atMost:)` on a tree of 32 levels and requires it to read at 32 and to throw `nestedTooDeeply(limit: 31)` at 31.
- To test a limit a test lifts or lowers it for one operation: `try MarkupGuard.$limits.withValue(MarkupGuard.Limits(deepestNesting: 32, mostMemoryUnits: .max, mostAttributesOnATag: .max, mostWork: .max)) { … }` binds a task-local, so the limits change only the reading that closure does on its own thread. `testTheStandardLimitsArePinned` pins 128, 1,000,000, 1,000, and 30,000,000,000; `testTheRefusalsSpeakPlainly` pins the four sentences; `testTheLimitsInForceReachTheRealPath` binds small limits and requires the converter, `HTMLParser`, and the guard's three parses to refuse.
- The reading paths are held through each format's own code: an EPUB reads around a refused chapter, a Kindle book, a DAISY navigation file, a web archive's page, feed discovery, and a feed's title and text meet the guard (`testAnEPUBReadsAroundWhatItsChaptersHold` and its neighbours).
- `testEveryParseGoesThroughTheGuard` reads the app's sources and fails on any SwiftSoup parse outside MarkupGuard.swift.
- The big stack (0.4.2) has its own tests: `testTheBigStackHoldsTheDeepestTreeThePassLetsThrough` (its size, a whole number of megabytes, from the measured cost of a level), `testTheBodyRunsOnAThreadWithTheBigStackAndItsResultComesBack`, a parse from a 512 KiB worker thread that gives the same tree as SwiftSoup alone in each of the three parsers, and `testTheSecondLayerHasALimitOfItsOwnWhenOneIsSet`, which sets `Limits.deepestTree` apart from `deepestNesting` on a tree of 60 levels.
- The policy test of the stack is `testEverySwiftSoupParseInTheGuardRunsOnTheBigStack`. It reads MarkupGuard.swift without its line comments and requires 3 `SwiftSoup.parse` calls, each the argument of `requireDepth(of:atMost:)` inside an `onLargeStack` closure, so that neither a parse nor a depth walk can leave the big stack, and a refused tree is released there. Its first form let a depth walk move out of the closure; the November 8 check found that. It does not strip block comments, and `testEveryParseGoesThroughTheGuard` matches fixed names only, so a typealias would evade it.
- `Scripts/test/markup-stack-proof.py` (with the programs in `Scripts/test/markup-stack-proof/measure` and `proof`) proves in child processes what the test host cannot, that the guard's parse does not overflow a stack:
  - `build` compiles the two programs against the committed MarkupGuard sources and the SwiftSoup of the last build, in debug, Release, or both.
  - `measure` parses a deep document on a 4 GiB stack and counts the pages touched, which gives the bytes a level costs each shape.
  - `proof` calls the guard from a 512 KiB thread with the first layer's limit lifted and the second layer's at 128, and requires the depth sentence and no crash. A control calls SwiftSoup alone and overflows.
  - It takes the heavy-work lock, writes to `build/markup-stack/` in the main checkout, and runs with `python3 -I -B`.
  - In Release, 22 of 23 cases passed on the first full run. The 23rd, a raw closed-span case 3,000 deep, was changed to 5,000 deep and run by hand: it overflows, which is the control's point. The whole script was not run again after that edit.
- A debug build converts only about 108 levels of lists and 117 of divisions on an import's 512 KiB stack, so no in-process test converts more than 40 levels. The tests of other readers that need a tree thousands of levels deep (deep navigation files in `EPUBHeadingRecoveryTests`, `EPUBPageListTests`, and `DAISYMalformedXMLTests`, 5,000 levels in two of them) lift only the depth, with `MarkupGuard.Limits.withoutDepthLimit` (defined at the top of MarkupGuardTests.swift) under `MarkupGuard.$limits.withValue`, on `onWorkerSizedStack`, to hold the reader's own walk to itself, since the guard would refuse such a file first.
- Proof that a document cannot crash is never made in the test host. A document whose count or parse is in doubt is first run in a harness in a child process with a stack of 512 KiB and memory and time limits, and only then written into a test; the runs over millions of units are made the same way (in the last round, 5,085,936 units at 40 repeats, and 2,154,267 read with 2,931,669 refused at 2,000, in Release, with 0 faults). `MarkupGuardTests` is in no area of Scripts/test/areas.conf yet, so run it by name.

## KFX tests
<!-- id: kfx-tests; keys: ; src: TextMouthProductionTests/KFXRecognitionTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/KFXContainerTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/KFXIonTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/KFXWalkTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/KFXNavigationTests.swift -->

The KFX tests are `KFXRecognitionTests`, `KFXContainerTests`, `KFXIonTests`, `KFXWalkTests`, `KFXNavigationTests`, `KFXSetTests`, `KFXInlineBookTests`, `KFXFixRoundTests`, and `KFXHostileFileTests`, on books that `KFXFixture` and `KFXTestBook` build in code, and `KFXEPUBComparisonTests`, which reads `kfx/kfx-features.kfx` and `kfx/alice.kfx` and requires the words and headings of the EPUB each was made from (and, for the feature book, its chapters and its 5 pages), with two named differences: the title of a chapter with no words, which the EPUB takes from the document's title and the KFX book does not have, and, in the inline book, the EPUB sets math apart as a block where the KFX book reads the author's name for it. The samples in `TestData/format-samples/kfx` are made with `Scripts/make-kfx-sample.py`, which writes a small EPUB with everything the walk meets and converts it with Amazon's Kindle Previewer and a packaging tool, both run as black boxes by a wrapper script in the main checkout's gitignored build folder. The reader was written from the format notes and the public Ion specification, not from either tool's code, and Previewer stamps a new identifier into every run, so the books' bytes differ at each run and their SHA-256 in `samples.tsv` is the sample in the repository. `KFXPrivateBooksTests` reads books that the developer places by hand in a gitignored folder of the main checkout, never committed, and reports only counts (books, words, headings, pages) and, for each refusal, its sentence; it skips when the folder is absent or empty, as it does in a worktree.

## Kindle samples without goldens

The Kindle samples in `TestData/format-samples/mobi` have no goldens. MOBIReaderTests reads two of them: Project Gutenberg's MOBI 6 book, and `ferry-notes-ncx.mobi`, a small book with a table of contents that `Scripts/make-mobi-ncx-sample.py` makes with calibre's `ebook-convert` (calibre writes a new identifier into every conversion, so the script's output never matches the sample byte for byte). ## KF8 tests
<!-- id: kf8-tests; keys: ; src: TextMouthProductionTests/KF8ReviewFixTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/KF8RealBookTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/PagesUpgradeTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/OnDiskContractTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/KF8LayoutTests.swift -->

The KF8 tests (FMT48, 0.4.2) read Kindle books in KF8 and are `KF8LayoutTests`, `KF8BookTests`, `KF8HostileFileTests`, `KF8ReviewFixTests`, and `KF8RealBookTests`, on books that `KF8Fixture` builds in code and on the samples below.

- `KF8LayoutTests` pin the tables, the end-to-end layout check, and the join. `KF8BookTests` pin the sections, titles, headings, pages, and metadata.
- `KF8ReviewFixTests` pin what the review asked for: the generated contents page and the real last page it must not take, fragments that go backward or leave a gap, the 1 KB label cap, the fallback from a hybrid's KF8 half to its MOBI 6 half in every way short of protection and a cancel, SVG words, navigation lists, the continuation rule, and a damaged contents or guide.
- The chapter rules of 0.4.3 (see the continuation rule in [Imports](../architecture/state/imports.md)) are pinned in three places:
  - `KF8RealBookTests.testAModestProposalReadsAsItsEPUBsThreeChaptersWithTheLicensesOpeningInTheLicense` reads the real pair: 3 chapters of 169, 3,386, and 2,906 words, with the titles, word counts, words, and headings of the EPUB. The samples are `mobi/pg1080-modest-proposal-kf8.mobi` (422,746 bytes) and `azw3/pg1080-modest-proposal.epub` (263,753 bytes), each under 500 KB and in `samples.tsv`. The Yellow Wallpaper's test now expects a start entry before the licence's heading.
  - `KF8ReviewFixTests.testAnEssayAfterAWholeHeaderIsItsOwnChapterAndALicenseKeepsItsOpeningWords` builds the same shape with `KF8Fixture`.
  - `KF8ReviewFixTests.testPiecesInTheSameSectionOrAfterAFooterOrBeforeAHeadingInAnotherSectionStillGoOn` holds what the rules leave alone: the review's two sections cut inside the second (one chapter ending "Second half."), a piece after a footer, and a plain `div` after a section (two chapters).
  - Breaking each rule in a scratch copy moved the real numbers: without the header rule Modest Proposal is 2 chapters; without the shared-container rule the licence's opening lands in the essay (3,599 and 2,693 words); without the continuation it is 4 chapters; without the footer exemption Pride and Prejudice is 67 chapters, not 66. The two-section cut was proven with a throwaway program, not an XCTest run. The `<a>` skip in `openingContainer` has no test.
- The soft hyphens (0.4.3) are pinned by `SoftHyphenEffectTests` (the measured effects on search and braille, see Shared helpers in [Architecture](../../ARCHITECTURE.md)), by `KF8ReviewFixTests.testEverySpellingOfASoftHyphenIsLeftOut` (every spelling `ParserUtilities.removingSoftHyphens` catches, and the ones it leaves alone: `&SHY;`, `&Shy;`, `&shy1`, `&shyx`, other numbers, and a huge one), and by `MOBIBookStructureTests` for a MOBI 6 book's words, labels, Windows-1252 text, and plain PalmDOC text. `MOBIReaderTests`, `PagesUpgradeTests`, and `OnDiskContractTests` expect the `.mobi` page reader line "1.1.0".
- `PortableXMLDifferentialTests` lists two known differences for the new EPUB, its `toc.xhtml` and `toc.ncx`: they write the title across two lines with `&#13;`, which Foundation's tidy reading keeps and the portable tree collapses. The strict reading, which the EPUB reader uses on both platforms, agrees. The evidence is the root element's text, so the two entries also pass any other tidy difference in those two files.
- `KF8HostileFileTests` damage a book so that a table, a count, a place, or a length lies, and require a typed refusal (or a read) in well under a second, with no trap. They now import through `MOBIParser.prepareBook`, so a section's markup meets the markup guard and the nesting and declaration inputs end in `MarkupRefusal.nestedTooDeeply` or a normal read. The flip and truncation tests import every fourth or eighth copy that opens.
- `KF8RealBookTests` read the samples in `TestData/format-samples/azw3` and `mobi` as an import does and compare each with the same book as an EPUB through the EPUB reader: the same words in the same order, the same headings, and the same pages, with the differences named in the test. The samples are Standard Ebooks' The Time Machine (`se-the-time-machine.azw3`), `quay-almanac` (an EPUB, an .azw3 and a calibre hybrid, made by `Scripts/make-kf8-samples.py`), Gutenberg's Yellow Wallpaper in KF8, Gutenberg's A Modest Proposal in KF8 with its EPUB (0.4.3), Gutenberg's Ghosts as kindlegen made it (a hybrid), and the edge books.
- The edge samples are in `TestData/format-samples/azw3/edge` (an EPUB and its .azw3 each, made with calibre) and `mobi/edge` (a calibre hybrid and a kindlegen hybrid each): `toc-last`, `toc-mixed`, `toc-epilogue`, `toc-single`, `page-list`, `svg-words`, `nav-in-spine`, and `page-break-style`. `Scripts/make-kf8-edge-samples.py` and `Scripts/make-kf8-edge-samples-2.py` write their EPUBs, every word of whose prose is a unique token, and convert them with calibre's `ebook-convert` and Amazon's kindlegen (Kindle Previewer 4's copy), both run as black boxes. Their bytes change at each conversion, so the SHA-256 in `samples.tsv` is the sample in the repository.
- Tests changed by KF8: the "KF8 is refused" tests of `MOBIReaderTests` and `MOBIBookStructureTests` now read a KF8 book or call a book without its tables damaged, `.azw3` is a Kindle extension in `testKindleBooksAreRecognizedByTheirExtensions` and the Finder test, and `PageReaderVersions.current(for: .mobi)` is "1.0.0" in `PagesUpgradeTests` and `OnDiskContractTests`.
- The hosted tests (the upgrade of a stored hybrid without a kept copy, the 70 MB picture-file test, the sparse 600 MB file refusal, the Info.plist and project checks) need the train.
- `Scripts/test/kf8-fuzz` (`build-reader.py`, `fuzz.py`, `Main.swift`, `Stubs.swift`) is a fuzz kit that damages books at random with a seed and reads each in a child process. It is not part of the suite. It was built and run in 0.4.3: the builder lacked five parser files and looked for packages in a build folder a worktree does not have, and now builds.
  - `build-reader.py OUTPUT [WORKFOLDER]` builds the reader program with `swiftc`, from the repository's sources and the packages an earlier Release build left in `build/DerivedData/Build/Products/Release`. In a worktree with none, set `KF8_FUZZ_BUILD` to another checkout's `build` folder. It is heavy: run it under `run_locked.py`. Its files are overwritten by every build.
  - The reader program has three modes. `reader kf8 BOOK` reads a book as an import does, and prints OK and counts, or ERR and the sentence a listener would hear. `reader sections BOOK` (an .azw3, a .mobi, or an .epub) prints each chapter's number, words, title, and first and last six words, each heading, the words' SHA-256, and the count of soft hyphens left, so two files of one book can be compared with `diff`. `reader sections6 BOOK` reads a hybrid's MOBI 6 half.
  - `fuzz.py --reader PATH --work FOLDER --seed N --rounds N BOOK...` changes one to four bytes of a copy of a book each round, half in the first 6 KB or the last 40 KB, and runs the reader with a time limit (`--seconds`, 5 by default) and a memory limit (`--megabytes`, 1024). A signal, a time over the limit, or a peak memory over it fails the round, and a trapping round's file is kept as `trap-<round>.bin`. It deletes nothing.
  - First runs, November 9, 2026, seed 1 and 2,000 rounds each: The Time Machine 1,736 read and 264 refused, the Quay Almanac hybrid 1,863 and 137, Yellow Wallpaper in KF8 1,830 and 170. Seed 2, 2,000 rounds over four books: 1,820 and 180. No trap, no slow or large round, and no SwiftSoup, `MarkupGuard`, or converter failure.
- The branch's train (0.4.2) ran 19 classes (the KF8, MOBI, and KFX classes, `MarkupGuardTests`, `PagesUpgradeTests`, `OnDiskContractTests`, `DocumentTypeDeclarationTests`, `IOSInfoPlistTests`, `OwnReaderCancellationTests`, and `EPUBExportRealBooksTests`) with `--areas import,feeds` and passed 2,894 tests with 7 skipped.

## Text without a byte-order mark
<!-- id: text-without-byte-order-mark; keys: ; src: TextMouthProductionTests/TextFileDecoderTests.swift, TextMouth/Models/TextEncodingChoice.swift, TextMouth/Parsers/TextFileDecoder.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/PagesUpgradeTests.swift, TextMouthProductionTests/OnDiskContractTests.swift -->

`TextFileDecoderTests` pin how text without a byte-order mark is read (0.4.3; see `unmarkedUnicode(in:)` under Imports in [Architecture](../../ARCHITECTURE.md)).

- `testUnmarkedUnicodeInEveryScriptIsRecognizedByItsBytes` and `testTheShortestUnmarkedTextsThatAreRecognized` pin what is found and how short it may be.
- Four tests pin what must not be taken for UTF-16 or UTF-32: `testBytesThatAreNotUnmarkedUnicodeAreNotReadAsIt`, `testTextInOtherEncodingsIsNeverTakenForUnmarkedUnicode` (the same sentences in more than 20 encodings), `testTheTextSamplesInTestDataAreNeverTakenForUnmarkedUnicode` (89 samples, about 124,000 windows), and the two sweeps with one to four NULs put in at random, `testTheTextSamplesInTestDataWithStrayNULsAreNeverTakenForUnmarkedUnicode` and `testStrayNULsDoNotTurnTextIntoUnmarkedUnicode`. The NUL sweeps are the ones that can fail: without a zero byte the rules for other scripts cannot fire, and the review found the first sweeps could not fail.
- `testARecordedEncodingAndTheLegacyEvidenceAskOnlyTheOldRule` pins that a book's recorded encoding, and the web archive and web-page .doc readers, ask for Latin UTF-16 alone. `testAUTF16ColumnOfSingleCharactersIsNotTakenForUTF32` pins a column of one character to a line.
- The ISO-2022-JP tests pin the declaration of an HTML file.
- Left unfound on purpose, and so untested as found: UTF-16 in the scripts of India, and Chinese, Japanese, or Korean UTF-16 with fewer than 8 spaces, digits, or line ends.
- The reader versions are plain text "1.0.1" and HTML "1.0.4", with EPUB still "1.0.3" (`PagesUpgradeTests`, `OnDiskContractTests`).

## MOBI tests on real books
<!-- id: mobi-tests-real-books; keys: ; src: TextMouthProductionTests/MOBIRealBookTests.swift -->

MOBIRealBookTests reads five real books from other makers, each pinned by the SHA-256 of its decoded text, which a decoder written apart from TextMouth's (a Python port of mobi.js) computed, and by its names and some of its words. Three are libmobi's test samples, under libmobi's LGPL-3.0, whose text and the GPL-3.0 it builds on are beside them as `libmobi-COPYING.LESSER` and `libmobi-COPYING`: a kindlegen book compressed with HUFF/CDIC, whose digest is also that of the same book stored uncompressed, so it proves the HUFF reading; a Windows-1252 book with a short header; and an early Mobipocket book whose PalmDOC text is HTML. The other two are Gutenberg's Ghosts as kindlegen made it, a book holding both MOBI 6 and KF8, and a book in Afrikaans in the older Mobipocket style, whose records carry two trailing entries. Of more than 40 real books checked when these were chosen, none decoded to less text than it declared, and two dictionaries decoded to 166 bytes more, which the reader cuts off. `samples.tsv` gives each one's source.
