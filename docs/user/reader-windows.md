# Reader windows

Part of [TextMouth's documentation](../../README.md).

- Each book opens in its own window, and several can be open at once.
- Just above the playback controls, the book's text, up to 20,000 characters of the chapter around the reading position, is in a scroll area VoiceOver calls Book content: interact with it to read the text with VoiceOver or on a braille display, whether the book is playing or paused. The part shown always holds the reading position, keeps whole paragraphs where it can, and never begins or ends inside a word, except in a language written without spaces. The area doesn't scroll to the reading position, so in a long chapter the text begins up to about 10,000 characters before it.
- In a library in the cloud, a book whose files are only in the cloud downloads in its reader window before it opens (see [Keeping your library in the cloud](cloud-library.md)).
- An open book follows what the library does to it elsewhere: a new title or author, a move to another folder, or a renamed folder. Nothing reloads and speech carries on; an audiobook playing while its files move stops for a moment and plays on from the same place, and one playing from its working copy in a library in the cloud doesn't stop at all. Meanwhile the book counts as playing: a Pause or Play/Pause, the sleep timer running out, a recording or another book starting, the AirPods leaving, a stop, or Release Media Controls during the move leaves it paused or stopped when the move is over, and Play then plays it from its place in the moved files. A sleep timer set to start playback, or Auto-play on navigate, leaves it to play on as the move ends, and Where Am I? pressed during the move lets it play on once the announcement has ended. The data directory itself can't move while any book is open.
- The Playback menu holds Play / Pause, Skip Forward and Skip Backward, Cycle Forward Navigation Unit and Cycle Backward Navigation Unit, Increase Speed and Decrease Speed, Where Am I? and Where Am I? Full Summary, then, while the window's book holds the media controls (below), Release Media Controls, and last Copy Reading Position (Shift-Command-P) and Search in Book (Command-F).
- Speak Info, Shift-Command-I, speaks this window's book's brief summary in the same words the Library's Speak Info says for its row, and Shift-Command-I pressed twice quickly speaks its full summary (since October 17, 2026). In a reader it is a VoiceOver announcement, made while the book goes on playing, so with VoiceOver off it says nothing; it has no menu item here, since the Library menu is hidden in a reader.
- The Navigate menu holds Go to Library, Go Back and Go Forward, Next Heading and Previous Heading, Next in Reading List and Previous in Reading List (below), Show Headings, Show Sleep Timer, and Book Info.
- Next Heading, Previous Heading, and the subheading jumps say every heading that begins where they land, the outermost first: a season's first episode says "Heading: Season Two, King Nine Will Not Return". Headings that begin at one place are one stop. Choosing a heading in Show Headings is unchanged.
- The Bookmarks menu holds Toggle Bookmark, Show Bookmarks, and Next Bookmark and Previous Bookmark.
- The reader's panels: in Show Headings, each heading's row offers Go To and Skip Section, or Include Section, among its actions in VoiceOver's Actions menu (VO-Command-Space), as its context menu does, and its hint says "Goes to this heading and closes the list."; the level filter, Show levels, offers Level 1, Levels 1 to 2, and so on, and All levels. In Show Bookmarks, a bookmark's row ends with the day it was added, as in "Bookmark 3, The Battle, Note: Check this, added October 9, 2026", which the Bookmarks rotor says too, and its hint says "Goes to this bookmark and closes the list."; with no bookmarks, the panel names the key that adds one, in words, as in "Command + B adds a bookmark at the current position.", or, while that command has no key, says "Add Bookmark adds one at the current position." Search in Book lists the first 500 matches, saying so when it finds more, and keeps every row of its list, so VoiceOver's cursor stays put in a long list. The status row's percent and time left are buttons, each opening a panel below it with one labelled field, Return to jump, and Escape to close it: the percent opens Go to Percent, whose field, "Percent through the book", starts at the place's percent; the time left opens Go to Time, whose field takes a time typed or pasted as 1:02:03, 62:03, 3723, or 12:04.3, and starts at the place's time, in whole seconds. For an audiobook the field is "Time in the recording": every time that names a place counts the recording's own time, whatever the speed, so a time from a chapter list, show notes, the audio editor, the Headings sheet, or Copy Reading Position lands where it says. For a text book it is "Time at 500 words a minute", at the book's own speed. A time less than a second before a heading lands on the heading. A time past the end is refused, as in "Time exceeds the recording's length. The recording lasts 9 hours, 41 minutes, 7 seconds.", shown under the field and spoken. Return, or Jump, while the field still holds the value it started at closes the panel as Escape does and leaves the place exactly where it is, saying nothing: the starting time drops the place's fraction of a second and the starting percent is rounded to a tenth, so jumping to either would move the place a little, and a finished book would no longer be finished. In Show Headings, Time In gives each heading's time on the same clock, for an audiobook the same at every speed, so it can be typed into Go to Time, and Duration gives each heading's length at the book's speed, to the next heading shown, the last to the very end of the book (a last chapter of exactly 25:03 shows 25:03). The sheet follows a change of speed, or of the setting that names the speed, made while it is open. Copy Reading Position adds the place's time, as in "at 2:13:05 of 9:41:07 in the recording", or for a text book "at 2:10:24 of 4:20:49 at 500 words a minute", which Go to Time takes back; a finished book copies "Finished" in place of its percent, time, and time left. A book's own settings open from the reader's toolbar. For a text book the button is Voice, which VoiceOver calls Change voice for this book, and its sheet, Voice & Speed for This Book, holds the book's voice, speed, and pitch and what happens when the book ends; there Save to Global Settings, which the playback bar offers too, makes the book's voice, speed, and pitch the defaults, and Reset to Global Settings removes all three from the book, saying "Using global voice, speed, and pitch settings". For an audiobook the button is Audio Settings, named the same on screen and by VoiceOver, and its sheet, Audio Settings for This Book, holds the book's speed, one slider VoiceOver reads as "Audio speed, 1.25 times speed", which moves by 0.05 times; what happens when the book ends; and Reset to Normal Speed while the book has a speed of its own.
- The Book menu holds Edit Text in Default Editor and Edit Text With…, available for a text book and a transcribed audiobook, but not a feed's episode or article, then Transcribe Audio…, available for an audiobook, Clean Up Transcript (Experimental)…, for a transcribed one that isn't a feed's episode, and Delete Transcript…, for a transcribed one, then Edit Audio…, for an audiobook other than a feed's episode (see [Editing audio](editing-audio.md)), and last Move to Library…, for an episode or article downloaded in its feed (see [Podcasts and article feeds](feeds.md)). File menu, Export… exports the reader's book.
- Headphone and media keys control the reader that is playing, or, when none is, whichever came last of the reader window you used and the book that paused, or reached its end, while playing; a Play command, which some headphones send apart from Play/Pause, goes to that last one even while another book plays. A book that pauses while another book's window is in front, from the AirPods, a media key, its sleep timer running out, the headphones disconnecting, a recording starting, or its voice not starting a passage (below), is shown paused in Now Playing, and the next Play resumes it rather than starting the book in front; moving to another book's window afterwards makes that window's book the one Play starts. A book waiting to play, for its chapter's speech or for a chapter's files to download, counts as playing: the AirPods leaving, a recording starting, or another book starting pauses it, so it never starts by itself once ready; Auto-play on navigate leaves such a book waiting. A book that ends and stops there, rather than starting again or continuing to the next book, rests finished at its end while its window stays open: Now Playing shows it paused at its end, so the AirPods and the media keys still reach TextMouth, and Previous Track or Skip Backward, from them or from the reader's own Skip Backward, moves back by the skip unit from the end and plays from there, so you can hear its last words again, with the skip's sound and then Play's. It plays again only while no other book plays: with another book playing, Skip Backward in the finished book's window moves its cursor back without playing, leaves the other book playing, and the book is finished no longer. When a sleep timer set for the end of the heading or of the book stops, at its end, a book that would continue to the next book, the timer is spent and the book rests finished: Skip Backward plays its end again, at full volume, and when it ends this time, the next book starts. Skip Forward or Next Track by a number of seconds stays at the end and plays nothing, while a forward skip by sentence, paragraph, heading, or bookmark that finds a later place moves there without playing. That move, or any other jump, such as to a heading, ends the finished state, as Release Media Controls does: the book is then an ordinary stopped book, whose skips move the cursor without playing, and Play plays from the cursor, or, while the cursor is still at the end, does what Play does at a book's end (see When a book ends, below). Closing the window, or quitting TextMouth, keeps it: the book is saved at its end, and opens finished again. A book that continues to the next book, in its folder or in the reading list, doesn't rest finished, though it too is saved at its end: as VoiceOver says "Starting: Dune", Now Playing shows it paused at its end until Dune plays, and a rewind pressed in that moment moves its cursor back without playing over the book announced. Play pressed in that moment, from the AirPods, a media key, or the keyboard, starts Dune at once instead of half a second later, and until Dune starts, Play never plays the finished book: once Dune has been asked for, Play or Play/Pause reaching the finished book says "Still starting: Dune"; once Dune plays, Play in the finished book's window plays it as usual. Release Media Controls is in the Playback menu while the window's book holds the media controls, meaning Now Playing shows it, playing or paused: a finished book counts, and so does a book whose speech stopped where the speech engine refused the text or where the sleep timer stopped it. It hands the controls back to the system and says "Media controls released". Once another book plays, that book holds them, and the first book's window no longer offers Release Media Controls; a key you gave it then changes nothing there and says "Media controls not released: this book does not hold them." Closing a book's window, or the book being stopped, as when a chapter can't be downloaded or its text is replaced, takes its book out of Now Playing whenever Now Playing shows it, even from behind another book's window; the book whose window you used last is shown instead if it was played and is paused, and otherwise the AirPods and the media keys go back to other apps. A Pause that pauses nothing, as one reaching a book already paused or finished, makes no pause sound, and one reaching a book that doesn't hold the media controls, such as one that never played, was stopped, or released them, tells the system nothing, so the AirPods stay with the app or the book they are with. Saving a book's metadata, in its window or the Library window, or any other change of its title or author, such as the metadata refresh after an update, shows its new title in Now Playing only while the book holds the media controls, a finished book included, so a save never takes the AirPods from the app or the book you were listening to.
- The sleep timer runs for a set number of minutes, a preset or typed, or stops at a place in the book: its sheet's Stop At section sets one that stops at the end of the heading, or at the end of the book, for text books and audiobooks alike. Headings are one concept for any book: where the next heading of any level begins, as Next Heading finds it, or the book ends, in a text book of any format or an audiobook, whose chapters, tracks, and chapter markers are its headings. End of heading pauses playback where the heading playing ends: within a chapter, where the next heading begins, with the cursor put back at that heading for Play to start it; at a chapter's end, when the next chapter opens with the next heading, as every audiobook chapter does, with the reader waiting at the start of that chapter, which Play starts; and at the book's end, which ends the last heading. A next chapter that has text before its first heading, or no heading at all, goes on under the same heading, so playback goes on into it. End of heading took the place of End of chapter on October 6, 2026, and Restart Sleep Timer after a timer saved as End of chapter, or as End of document, arms End of heading. VoiceOver says "Sleep timer: stops at the end of this heading." as the timer is set, reads its status the same way in the sheet and in the reader, and says "Sleep timer expired at the end of the heading. Playback paused." where it stops; stopped at the book's end, the reader says instead that the book finished and why nothing follows, as in "Finished: Moby-Dick. Next book not started because of the sleep timer." End of book pauses playback when the book ends, before the book starts again or the next book starts. An audiobook fades out toward a stop at a chapter's end over the sleep timer's Fade out time, in Playback settings, when one is set; speech is not faded toward such a stop, and an m4b's chapter marker inside one file stops the timer a moment past the marker, without a fade, the cursor put back at the marker. When a timer counting minutes runs out and a Fade out time is set, playback fades out over that time before it pauses: an audiobook in its own audio, speech playing from the audio TextMouth prepared in that audio, and speech read straight from the voice in ever quieter stretches, the voice starting again from the word it had reached. Since October 10, 2026, speech read straight from the voice no longer moves to the prepared audio as the fade begins, a move that could repeat or skip a few words. One timer serves the whole app, and it belongs to the reader window that set it: another window's playback, even of the same book, never pauses or ends it. In the sheet, the custom timer's field and its Adjust minutes stepper both say their minutes with the unit, as in "30 minutes", and the stepper moves by 5; a custom timer of one minute is said in the singular, as in "Set timer for 1 minute and start playing".
- When a book ends, TextMouth stops, starts the book again, or continues to the next book in the folder, as Playback settings say under When book ends: Stop, the default; Stop, and Play Starts Over; Loop to Start; or Continue to Next Book. Four settings can decide, the first that applies winning:
  - First, a book read from the reading list follows the list instead (below).
  - Second, each book can override that choice in its own settings (above). The settings sheet's picker, When this book ends, which VoiceOver calls "End of book behavior for this book", names the choice in force, and names Continue to Next Book whether the book's feed or folder plays the book below or the one above. Use Global Default, which VoiceOver calls "Reset to global end-of-book setting" and which shows only while the book has a choice of its own, removes the book's own choice, so that the choice of its feed or folder applies, or the global setting where it has none.
  - Third, a feed and a plain folder each choose for their own books with a pop-up button, "When an episode ends" in Feed Info and "When a book ends" in Folder Settings (see [Podcasts and article feeds](feeds.md) and [The Library window](library-window.md)). Its choices are Play the book below, which is Continue to Next Book; Play the book above, which is Continue to Next Book going up the list or folder instead of down; Stop; and Use the global setting, which leaves the choice to the Playback tab. A feed says episode in place of book, or article or item when its items are those. A folder's choice applies to the books directly in it, never to those in the folders inside it, and the top of the library has none.
  - Last, the global setting, When book ends in the Playback tab, decides.
  - Skip finished, which a folder or a feed keeps for itself (Skip finished books in a folder, Skip finished episodes in a feed), applies whichever setting decides, and is checked unless it was cleared there; a book read from the reading list follows its list's own Skip finished books instead. Continue to Next Book, and a choice to play the book below or above, pass over the books that rest at their end, finished, and go on with the first beyond them that is not; with Skip finished cleared, they go on with the very next book, and a finished book they go on to plays from its beginning, after "Starting: <title>" as for any book. When only finished books lie that way, the book says so, as in "Finished: Dune. Every book after it in the folder is finished.", or, going up, "Finished: Dune. Every book before it in the folder is finished."; and with none that way, "Finished: Dune. No more books in folder." A feed's episodes and articles say the same words.
  - For a feed's episode or article, the next one is the next one its feed lists that way, newest first, downloaded or not (see Listening on to the next episode, in [Podcasts and article feeds](feeds.md)). One that is not on this Mac is downloaded first, and that download goes first in the queue. VoiceOver says "Finished: Ep. 368. Downloading the next episode: Ep. 367." ("the next article" for an article), and "Starting: Ep. 367" when the file arrives. Until it arrives, the episode rests finished at its end, as a book that stops does (above). If the download fails, VoiceOver says "The next episode could not be downloaded, so playback stopped." followed by the download's own sentence, as the episode's row says it; if TextMouth will not start the download, as while it quits, that sentence is "TextMouth is not downloading right now." If you close the window before the file arrives, nothing starts when it does. Nothing starts either, and nothing is said of the download, if you have moved on in another way: played the episode again, or taken the media controls for another book, by playing it or by bringing its window to the front. Pressing Play on the finished episode ends the wait too, since Play does what the end does under the options in force then, which may be to wait again. A sleep timer you start in the window while it waits keeps the episode from starting when it arrives, and VoiceOver says "Finished: Ep. 368. Next book not started because of the sleep timer." If you cancel that download yourself, with Cancel Download on its row, the Cancel of the Feed Downloads section, or Delete Feed…, VoiceOver says "Download cancelled, so playback stopped." and the episode stays finished; Play on it, which does what its end did, asks for the download again.
  - With a sleep timer in effect in the window, no next book starts, and no download for one either: VoiceOver says "Finished: Dune. Next book not started because of the sleep timer." (see the sleep timer, above).
- Unless it loops, a book that plays or is read to its end is saved exactly at its end, its last chapter at its full length, and stays finished after its window closes or TextMouth quits. A finished book whose last chapter has grown shorter since, as when its text is read anew or edited, opens at its new end, still finished, as its row says; a place saved past the end of any other chapter opens at that chapter's end. A finished book's library row says "Finished" in place of its percent and time left, as in "Dune, by Frank Herbert, Finished, EPUB", and so do Speak Info, Where Am I?, and Copy Reading Position, while the reader's own status row keeps its percent and time. Play on a finished book does what its end did, without playing the end again: with Stop it says "Finished: Dune" and plays nothing; with Stop, and Play Starts Over, which stops at the end as Stop does, it starts the book over from its beginning, saying "Starting again: Dune"; with Continue to Next Book it goes on to the next book not finished (the very next book where Skip finished is cleared), or says why none follows, as the end did; with Loop to Start it plays the book again; a book with no choice of its own whose feed or folder has one does what that choice says (above); and a book read from the reading list does what the list's own When a book ends says (below). A caption under the Playback tab's picker says what Play does with the first two. A backward skip after the end replays the end (above), which counts as no new finish, and a forward skip at the end stays there. Go to Percent with 100 lands just short of the end, so Play then plays the last moment and ends the book again.
- TextMouth keeps the words around each place it saves for you (your reading position, each bookmark, and each heading you skip), so they can be found again when a book's text is read anew. Places saved before this version take their words from each book as it reads now: when the book next opens, and for the others once at launch, before any text is rebuilt. Nothing is spoken or shown; an audiobook's places count time and need none.
- Your places follow their words when a book's text changes. When TextMouth reads a book's text again, after an update changes how it reads books or after Redo Text Extraction, your reading position, bookmarks, and skipped sections stay with their words. When a book's text changes in any other way, they still do: each time you open a book, TextMouth checks every saved place, and finds one whose words have moved. When you edit a book's text with Edit Text, your places follow each edit too, and an open reader's cursor stays on the words it was at. All of this is silent. A skipped section the new text no longer has is kept aside and skips nothing, until a later text brings it back. A place whose words can't be found anywhere stays where it was saved, with its words, so a later version of the text that has them again can still find it.
- A book you open from the Reading List, by its row, its Open item, or Return, is read from the list. Its window is titled "Reading List: Moby-Dick by Herman Melville" in place of "Book: Moby-Dick by Herman Melville", the line under its title says where it stands, as in "Reading list, book 3 of 12", and the full Where Am I? adds the same words. When it ends, the list's When a book ends decides what follows, in place of the book's own end-of-book setting: the book below it, or above it, as the option says, opens and plays, read from the list too, passing over finished books while Skip finished books is checked, as in "Starting: Dune"; or the book stops, and VoiceOver says why, as in "Finished: Moby-Dick. End of the reading list.", "Finished: Moby-Dick. The reading list is set to stop when a book ends.", or "Finished: Moby-Dick. Every book below it in the reading list is finished." Either way the book counts as finished. A sleep timer set in that window keeps the next book from starting, as it does in a folder. Navigate menu, Next in Reading List and Previous in Reading List open the book below or above, passing over finished books the same way, and play it when this book was playing, as in "Starting: Dune", unless a sleep timer set in that window is in effect, as in "Opening: Dune. Not started because of the sleep timer."; from a book that wasn't playing, they open the next without playing it, as in "Opening: Dune". With no book that way they say so, as in "No next book in the reading list." They are dimmed for a book not read from the list, and have no keys until you give them some in the Shortcuts tab of Settings, under Navigation. A book opened any other way, from another category, Continue Listening, or Finder, reads as before, even when it is in the list. A book taken off the list while it is open, or removed from the library, goes back to its own end-of-book setting, and putting it back on the list doesn't change that until you open it from the Reading List again.
- A book's text can hold what looks like markup, such as the <sm> and </sm> one novel marks its silent messages with, and the speech engine of macOS 26.3.1 takes such tags for speech markup: it skipped the rest of a passage after an opening tag, and refused a passage holding a closing tag alone, after which the book's speech stopped and couldn't be started again. Since October 7, 2026, TextMouth hands the voice each tag with its two angle brackets changed to single angle quotation marks, which the engine doesn't take for markup, so the brackets are silent and what is between them, a tag's name included, is spoken. The engine takes for an opening tag any name of letters, figures, or symbols between angle brackets, with no space or slash inside it and spaces around it or not, such as <sm>, < sm >, <5>, <...>, <=>, or <*>; such a name followed by name=value attributes, as in <a href="x">; and empty brackets, <> or < >. Each of these made it skip the rest of a passage without a sound, and TextMouth silences the brackets of every one, and of every closing tag, such as </sm>. A less-than or greater-than sign that isn't part of a tag, as in "x < y and y > z", is still read aloud, except in audio TextMouth prepared again with every angle bracket silent (below), an ampersand is left for the voice, which reads it as "and", and the text on screen and on a braille display is unchanged. A null character (U+0000), which a badly converted file can hold, made the speech engine drop the rest of a passage without a sound, and without a refusal TextMouth could hear; since October 10, 2026 TextMouth hands the voice a space in its place. No other control character dropped anything. Where Am I?, which can quote the book, and Speak Info, which speaks titles, are spoken the same way. Should the speech engine still refuse a passage of a text book, TextMouth starts the voice afresh at once, since the one that refused would never speak again. If TextMouth has finished preparing the chapter's audio in the background, as it does while it speaks any chapter of up to 100,000 characters, speech goes on from that audio at the refused passage, from the last word heard in it or from its start, so nothing heard is heard twice. If the speech engine refuses the chapter's text as TextMouth prepares its audio in the background, TextMouth prepares the audio once more with every angle bracket silent, so that a refusal while the voice reads has that audio to go on from; speech played from that audio says no less-than or greater-than sign. Without prepared audio to go on from, TextMouth tries the passage once more from there, with every angle bracket in the rest of the passage silent, so a less-than sign there isn't read; the passages after it are read as before. That one more try belongs to the place: once speech has gone on from it, a refusal met at that same place later, as after a jump back to it, is tried once more too. Refused there again, with no prepared audio to go on from, speech stops with the cursor at that passage, and the book's window shows a sheet headed "Speech Stopped", with one sentence under the heading, which VoiceOver reads with it: "The Mac's speech engine refused to speak the text here, twice, so speech stopped. Play tries again from the same place." Below them, a read-only text area labelled Details, which VoiceOver reads line by line and a braille display pans, holds a report for finding the cause: the book and its format; the place, as the brief Where Am I? says it, then the chapter, and the character where speech stopped, counted both in the book's text and in the spoken text, whose offsets the speech log gives; how many times that place was refused while the window has been open; the time, as the logs write it; up to 100 characters before where speech stopped and 400 from it, of the book's text and of the text exactly as it was handed to the speech engine, after the substitutions and the markup guard, with where the refused text begins and ends said in words on lines of their own; the markup guard in force; the voice, the speed, and the rate, pitch, and volume handed to the engine; macOS's version; TextMouth's build; and the last 40 lines the book's speech logged, including the routine lines that the speech log's file keeps only while tracing is on, which hold offsets and lengths, never the book's words. Copy Details copies the whole report as plain text and says "Details copied"; Show Logs in Finder opens the folder the logs are written to, and says why, aloud and under the buttons, when Finder can't; and Close, Return, or Escape closes the sheet. With the book's window in front, VoiceOver moves to the sheet's heading and hears nothing else. When another window or app is in front, a panel such as Headings or Bookmarks, the Export dialog, a transcription dialog, or an error alert is open over the book, or the report is open already, VoiceOver says instead "Speech stopped: the speech engine refused this passage. Details are in the book's window.", and the sheet waits in the book's window, or until what is over the book closes, when VoiceOver moves to it. A refusal while the report is open updates it in place, and Play leaves it open. TextMouth treats that stop as a pause: the AirPods and the media keys stay with TextMouth, and their next press is Play, which speaks from that passage; no time after the stop counts as listening; the place is saved; and a sleep timer's countdown waits until Play. Pressing Play there usually brings the same refusal and the report again, counting one more refusal of the place, so move past the passage first, with a forward skip by sentence or paragraph, or with Next Heading or Show Headings, and then press Play. A passage refused just as you pause is tried again, the same way, when you press Play, and one refused while the sleep timer fades speech out is tried again at once, no louder than the fade had come to. Should the speech engine stop a Where Am I? announcement before its end, the announcement ends without its end sound and the book stays paused, as a pause leaves it, so Play/Pause plays it at once, and the next Where Am I? speaks with a fresh start of its voice. Should it stop a Speak Info summary, the next press speaks, with a fresh start of the voice. Should the voice be handed a passage and not start it, TextMouth notices about five and a half seconds later, or about ten and a half while the voice has started nothing yet, as at the first Play in a book's window or after it was started afresh, since a voice still loading can take longer, counting only while speech should be heard; it then starts the voice afresh and goes on from the start of that passage, from the audio it prepared when it has it, and otherwise from the voice. A jump, or such a recovery, that would go on from the prepared audio goes on from the voice when the Mac can't play that audio. If the voice still hasn't started the passage after two fresh starts, speech pauses there, with no pause sound, the AirPods' next press is Play, and VoiceOver says "Speech paused: the voice did not start. Press Play to try again." Play resumes from that place, as after any pause. A voice that starts a passage and then goes silent without finishing it is started again from about where it went silent, at most twice at the same word; the third time speech pauses there in the same way, with the same words.
- Book menu: Edit Manifest in Default Editor and Edit Manifest With… open an audiobook of tracks' manifest.json in your editor (see [The audiobook manifest](audiobook-manifest.md#editing-an-imported-books-manifest)); they are unavailable for any other book.
- If an audiobook's audio stops moving while TextMouth shows it playing, TextMouth stops it after about ten seconds and says: "Playback stopped: the audio stopped responding. Press Play to try again; if it stops again, quit and reopen TextMouth."
- If TextMouth's memory grows past 3 GB while a book plays, which only a fault in macOS's audio causes, playback stops and TextMouth says how large its memory grew.
- TextMouth reads its own memory every 5 seconds while a book plays, and every 30 seconds when nothing does, or every 5 once it holds 1 GB or more. Past 6 GB, whether or not anything is playing and whatever the cause, it saves your place and quits, so a runaway can't exhaust the Mac's memory. The limit is 6 GB on every Mac, however much memory the Mac has.
- The next launch tells you of such a quit in an alert titled "TextMouth Quit to Protect Your Mac", with an OK button. The alert gives the date and time in your region's format and how large the memory had grown.
