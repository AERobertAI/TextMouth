# Reader windows

Part of [TextMouth's documentation](../../README.md).

- Each book opens in its own window, and several can be open at once.
- Just above the playback controls, the book's text, up to 20,000 characters of the chapter around the reading position, is in a scroll area VoiceOver calls Book content: interact with it to read the text with VoiceOver or on a braille display, whether the book is playing or paused. The part shown always holds the reading position, keeps whole paragraphs where it can, and never begins or ends inside a word, except in a language written without spaces. The area doesn't scroll to the reading position, so in a long chapter the text begins up to about 10,000 characters before it.
- In a library in the cloud, a book whose files are only in the cloud downloads in its reader window before it opens (see [Keeping your library in the cloud](cloud-library.md)).
- An open book follows what the library does to it elsewhere: a new title or author, a move to another folder, or a renamed folder. Nothing reloads and speech carries on; an audiobook playing while its files move stops for a moment and plays on from the same place, and one playing from its working copy in a library in the cloud doesn't stop at all. Meanwhile the book counts as playing: a Pause or Play/Pause, the sleep timer running out, a recording or another book starting, the AirPods leaving, a stop, or Release Media Controls during the move leaves it paused or stopped when the move is over, and Play then plays it from its place in the moved files. Play pressed during the move waits in the same way: the book shows Playing, and the move's end plays it from its place in the moved files, unless a Pause or a stop comes first. A sleep timer set to start playback, or Auto-play on navigate, leaves it to play on as the move ends, and Where Am I? pressed during the move lets it play on once the announcement has ended. The data directory itself can't move while any book is open.
- The Playback menu holds Play / Pause, Skip Forward and Skip Backward, Cycle Forward Navigation Unit and Cycle Backward Navigation Unit, Increase Speed and Decrease Speed, Where Am I? and Where Am I? Full Summary, then, while the window's book holds the media controls (below), Release Media Controls, and last Copy Reading Position (Shift-Command-P) and Search in Book (Command-F; see below).
- Speak Info, Shift-Command-I, speaks this window's book's brief summary in the same words the Library's Speak Info says for its row, and Shift-Command-I pressed twice quickly speaks its full summary (since October 17, 2026). In a reader it is a VoiceOver announcement, made while the book goes on playing, so with VoiceOver off it says nothing; it has no menu item here, since the Library menu is hidden in a reader.
- The Navigate menu holds Go to Library, Go Back, which in a reader returns to where the last jump began (see below), and Go Forward, Next Heading and Previous Heading, Go to Page… (Pages, below), Next in Reading List and Previous in Reading List (below), Next in Folder and Previous in Folder (below), Show Headings, Show Sleep Timer, and Book Info.
- Next Heading, Previous Heading, and the subheading jumps say every heading that begins where they land, the outermost first: a season's first episode says "Heading: Season Two, King Nine Will Not Return". Headings that begin at one place are one stop. Choosing a heading in Show Headings is unchanged.
- Pages: a book can have pages, numbered as its source numbers them, such as 12, xii, or A-3 ([What TextMouth reads](what-textmouth-reads.md) says which books have them). A page is a place in the book's text and adds no words: nothing is read aloud for a page, and moving by page moves no saved place. A page begins at its first whole word.
  - In an EPUB or HTML book, the number of a page break inside a heading is no part of the heading, as it is no part of the text: a heading written "Part 9Two", with the page break's number between the words, is the heading "Part Two" in the text, in Show Headings, and for Next Heading, and page 9 begins where the heading does.
  - Page is a navigation unit, between Paragraph and Heading in the list of units. With Page chosen, Skip Forward and Skip Backward move by page, and Cycle Forward Navigation Unit and Cycle Backward Navigation Unit stop at it. A book without pages has none to move by: the cycle passes Page over there, and Skip Forward or Skip Backward with Page chosen says "This book has no pages." and moves nothing. Neither has a book of only one page, which has no other page to go to: there the pickers leave Page out, and a skip by Page says "This book has only one page." and moves nothing.
  - For a book without pages, the playback bar's navigation unit pickers, the one VoiceOver calls Navigation unit or the two called Forward navigation unit and Backward navigation unit, leave Page out of their lists, unless Page is the unit in force, which stays in the list so that the picker shows its value. The four skip unit pickers in the Playback tab of Settings (see [Settings](settings.md)) list Page for every book.
  - With Page chosen, Skip Forward moves to the start of the next page and says its label, as in "Page 13", and Skip Backward moves to the start of the page being read, or to the start of the one before it when the reading position is within 10 characters of that start, and says its label. On the last page Skip Forward does nothing, and at the start of the first page, or before it, Skip Backward does nothing. Pages that begin at one place are one stop, and the label said is the last of them. A page that begins in a section you skip, or after the book's last word, is passed over. Each move keeps the place it began at for the toolbar's Return button, which VoiceOver calls "Return to previous reading position".
  - Go to Page… (Option-Command-G) opens a panel below the status row with one field, labelled "Page", whose hint says how many pages the book has and what to type, as in "This book has 300 pages, from i to 300. Type a page as the book numbers it, such as 12 or xii." (a book of one page names it: "This book's only page is 1.") The field starts at the label of the page being read, empty before the first page, and has the keyboard. Return, or Jump, goes to the start of the page typed and says "Jumped to page 12."; Escape, or Cancel, closes the panel. Capitals and spaces around the page don't matter: "XII" is page xii. A label that more than one page has, such as "2" on the plates and in the text, goes to the first such page after the page being read, or to the first in the book when none comes after it, and the jump says which of them it reached, counting from the first in book order: "Jumped to page 2, the second page labelled 2.", or "Jumped to page 2, the first page labelled 2." after the wrap. The count is in words up to the tenth and in figures after, as in "the 11th page labelled 2". Return while the field still holds the label it started at closes the panel, leaving the place where it is and saying nothing, unless another page of the book carries that label too: then Return goes to that label's next page, and after the last page that carries it, to the first, so Return on the starting label steps through the pages of a repeated label.
  - When the cursor does not land on the page asked for, Go to Page says where it is, and why when the book's pages say, in place of "Jumped to page 7.", so that its words agree with the status row:
    - A page with no words of its own, which begins where the page after it does: "Page 7 has no words, so the cursor is on page 8." A last page that begins after the book's last word is reached at that word, on the page before it: "Page 9 has no words, so the cursor is on page 8." When no page comes before, as in a book whose only page begins after its last word, the cursor is before the page: "Page 1 has no words, so the cursor is before page 1."
    - A page whose every word is in a skipped section, when the book is playing, since speech goes on with the next words it reads: "Page 40 is in a skipped section, so the cursor is on page 52." With the book stopped, the cursor stays on such a page, as on any other, and the jump says "Jumped to page 40."
    - No reason in the pages, as when a text substitution deletes every word of the page: "The cursor is on page 52."
    - A page of a label that other pages carry too is named as which of them it is, and so is the page the cursor is on, as in "The first page labelled 3 has no words, so the cursor is on the second page labelled 3.", "Page 5 has no words, so the cursor is on the second page labelled 2.", "The second page labelled 2 has no words, so the cursor is on page 3.", and "The second page labelled 2 is in a skipped section, so the cursor is on page 3."
    - A skip by Page says the label of the page the cursor reaches, as before.
  - Go to Page… refuses what is not a page, under the field and aloud, and leaves the panel open: "Type a page number." for an empty field; "There is no page 400 in this book. Its pages run from i to 300." for a page the book does not have; and, for a book of one page, "There is no page 400 in this book. Its only page is 1."
  - In a book without pages, the Navigate menu dims Go to Page…, and its key says "This book has no pages." and does nothing. In a book of only one page the menu dims it too, its key says "This book has only one page." and does nothing, and Where Am I? leaves the page out. While a book is still loading, Go to Page… and Skip Forward or Skip Backward with Page chosen do nothing and say nothing, since the book may have pages.
  - The status row has a button beside the percent that shows the page being read, as in "Page 12 of 300", or "Before page i" before the first page; VoiceOver reads the same words as its label. The N of "of N" is the largest page label that is a whole number, so a book numbered i to xiv and then 1 to 300 says "of 300", and a book with no such label counts its pages. Pressing the button opens Go to Page's panel, and its hint says what a press does: "Opens jump to page controls", "Hides jump to page controls" while that panel is open, and "Switches to jump to page controls" while the percent or time panel is. The button is drawn in a book of more than one page while "Show the page on the reader screen" is on (see [Settings](settings.md)), since a book of one page would say "Page 1 of 1" wherever you are; Go to Page… opens the panel either way. A page the book names "Page 12" is page 12, so the button never says "Page Page 12".
  - Where Am I? says the page right after the heading number, as in "Dune. The Battle. Heading 26 of 412. Page 112 of 300. 30 percent. 3 hours remaining."; Where Am I? Full Summary says it in the same place, and before the first page both say "Before page" and its label, as in "Before page i". "Say the page in Where Am I?" (see [Settings](settings.md)) turns it off.
- The Bookmarks menu holds Toggle Bookmark, Show Bookmarks, and Next Bookmark and Previous Bookmark. Next Bookmark and Previous Bookmark go in the order the bookmarks lie in the book, by chapter and then by place in the chapter, not in the order you made them, so with bookmark 1 in chapter 10 and bookmark 2 in chapter 3, Next Bookmark from chapter 1 lands on bookmark 2 first. Each still says its own number, as in "Bookmark 2", and a press with nothing beyond the cursor neither moves nor speaks. Add Bookmark and Toggle Bookmark say the number of the bookmark they made, as in "Bookmark 4 added", the number its row in Show Bookmarks has, and not how many bookmarks the book holds. When the book has left the library, as when it was removed while its reader stayed open, nothing is added or removed, and VoiceOver says, once focus has settled, "Could not add the bookmark: “Moby-Dick” is no longer in the library." or "Could not remove the bookmark: “Moby-Dick” is no longer in the library."
- The reader's panels: in Show Headings, each heading's row offers Go To and Skip Section, or Include Section, among its actions in VoiceOver's Actions menu (VO-Command-Space), as its context menu does, and its hint says "Goes to this heading and closes the list."; the level filter, Show levels, offers Level 1, Levels 1 to 2, and so on, and All levels. In Show Bookmarks, a bookmark's row ends with the day it was added, as in "Bookmark 3, The Battle, Note: Check this, added October 9, 2026", which the Bookmarks rotor says too, and its hint says "Goes to this bookmark and closes the list."; with no bookmarks, the panel names the key that adds one, in words, as in "Command + B adds a bookmark at the current position.", or, while that command has no key, says "Add Bookmark adds one at the current position." The status row's percent and time left are buttons, each opening a panel below it with one labelled field, Return to jump, and Escape to close it: the percent opens Go to Percent, whose field, "Percent through the book", starts at the place's percent; the time left opens Go to Time, whose field takes a time typed or pasted as 1:02:03, 62:03, 3723, or 12:04.3, and starts at the place's time, in whole seconds. For an audiobook the field is "Time in the recording": every time that names a place counts the recording's own time, whatever the speed, so a time from a chapter list, show notes, the audio editor, the Headings sheet, or Copy Reading Position lands where it says. For a text book it is "Time at 500 words a minute", at the book's own speed. A time less than a second before a heading lands on the heading. A time past the end is refused, as in "Time exceeds the recording's length. The recording lasts 9 hours, 41 minutes, 7 seconds.", shown under the field and spoken. Return, or Jump, while the field still holds the value it started at closes the panel as Escape does and leaves the place exactly where it is, saying nothing: the starting time drops the place's fraction of a second and the starting percent is rounded to a tenth, so jumping to either would move the place a little, and a finished book would no longer be finished. In Show Headings, Time In gives each heading's time on the same clock, for an audiobook the same at every speed, so it can be typed into Go to Time, and Duration gives each heading's length at the book's speed, to the next heading shown, the last to the very end of the book (a last chapter of exactly 25:03 shows 25:03). The sheet follows a change of speed, or of the setting that names the speed, made while it is open. Copy Reading Position adds the place's time, as in "at 2:13:05 of 9:41:07 in the recording", or for a text book "at 2:10:24 of 4:20:49 at 500 words a minute", which Go to Time takes back; a finished book copies "Finished" in place of its percent, time, and time left. A book's own settings open from the reader's toolbar. For a text book the button is Voice, which VoiceOver calls Change voice for this book, and its sheet, Voice & Speed for This Book, holds the book's voice, speed, and pitch and what happens when the book ends; there Save to Global Settings, which the playback bar offers too, makes the book's voice, speed, and pitch the defaults, and Reset to Global Settings removes all three from the book, saying "Using global voice, speed, and pitch settings". For an audiobook the button is Audio Settings, named the same on screen and by VoiceOver, and its sheet, Audio Settings for This Book, holds the book's speed, one slider VoiceOver reads as "Audio speed, 1.25 times speed", which moves by 0.05 times; what happens when the book ends; and Reset to Normal Speed while the book has a speed of its own.
- Search in Book, in the Playback menu (Command-F) and as the toolbar's Search button, which VoiceOver calls "Search in book", opens a sheet with a search field, "Search text", a Match Case checkbox, unchecked unless you check it, and a regular-expression switch, and lists the matches as you type: the first 500, saying so when it finds more, in a list that keeps every row, so VoiceOver's cursor stays put in a long list. For a text book each result gives its words in context, the heading it is under, and an estimate of where it is, as a percentage or a time as the Display picker chooses, as in "a drowned harbour, The Battle, at 12.5 percent of book". For an audiobook that has a transcript, Search in Book searches the transcript's text, and every result is timed exactly: the field's hint is "Type to search the book's transcript", the sheet has no Display picker, and a result's row shows when it is heard, as 1:03:45 or, under an hour, 3:45. VoiceOver reads the row, and the Search Results rotor lists it, as "a drowned harbour, The Battle, at 1 hour, 3 minutes, 45 seconds", the chapter named only when it has a title of its own that is not the book's title.
- Search Library, in the Edit menu's Find submenu (Option-Command-F), searches every book at once and opens a match in its reader, at those words, saying "Moved to the match, Chapter 3." once the window is in front; Go Back (below) returns to where the reader was before (see [Search Library](search-library.md)).
- Choosing a result of a transcript, by Return, Space, a click, or VoiceOver's activation, closes the sheet and moves the audio to the start of the sentence that holds the match, and with Auto-play on navigate on, a paused book plays on from there, as after any jump from a panel. Once the sheet has closed, VoiceOver says where the audio moved to, as in "Moved to 1 hour, 3 minutes, 45 seconds, The Battle." or, with no chapter to name, "Moved to 1 hour, 3 minutes, 45 seconds." Return chooses a focused result in a text book's list too.
- Go Back (Command-[), in the Navigate menu, and the toolbar's Return button, which VoiceOver calls "Return to previous reading position" and which is there only while there is a place to return to, take a reader back to where its last jump began, whether the jump was to a search result, a heading, a bookmark, a page, a percent, or a time. VoiceOver says "Returned to previous position." Only the last jump is remembered, and Go Back with no place to return to says "No previous position to return to."
- Search in Book on an audiobook with no text to search says why, in place of opening, and the toolbar shows no Search button:
  - With no transcript: "Moby-Dick has no transcript to search. Transcribe it first, with Transcribe Audio." For a book that is not in a package of its own, which can't be transcribed, it is only "Moby-Dick has no transcript to search."
  - With a transcript whose text is not shown: "Moby-Dick has no transcript text to search now", then, by the reason:
    - a full stop alone, when the reason isn't known;
    - the transcript is only in iCloud: ". The book's transcript is only in iCloud, so TextMouth leaves it as it is. Download the book, then try again.", or, when its text is: ". The book's text is only in iCloud, so TextMouth leaves it as it is. Download the book, then try again.";
    - the audio changed since the book was transcribed: ", because the audiobook's audio changed since it was transcribed, so its transcript no longer fits it. Transcribe it again, then search the new transcript.";
    - an edit of its text that TextMouth can't take: ", because its edited text can't be used: ", the reason, which ends "and the text shows", and where the edited text was kept (see [Editing a book's text](editing-book-text.md)).
- The Book menu holds Edit Text in Default Editor and Edit Text With…, available for a text book and a transcribed audiobook, a feed's episode included, but not a feed's article, then Transcribe Audio…, available for an audiobook and named Retranscribe Audio… once it has a transcript, Clean Up Transcript (Experimental)… and Delete Transcript…, for a transcribed one, then Edit Audio…, for an audiobook other than a feed's episode (see [Editing audio](editing-audio.md)), and last Move to Library…, for an episode or article downloaded in its feed (see [Podcasts and article feeds](feeds.md)). File menu, Export… exports the reader's book.
- Headphone and media keys control the reader that is playing, or, when none is, whichever came last of the reader window you used and the book that paused, or reached its end, while playing; a Play command, which some headphones send apart from Play/Pause, goes to that last one even while another book plays. A book that pauses while another book's window is in front, from the AirPods, a media key, its sleep timer running out, the headphones disconnecting, a recording starting, or its voice not starting a passage (below), is shown paused in Now Playing, and the next Play resumes it rather than starting the book in front; moving to another book's window afterwards makes that window's book the one Play starts. A book waiting to play, for its chapter's speech or for a chapter's files to download, counts as playing: the AirPods leaving, a recording starting, or another book starting pauses it, so it never starts by itself once ready; Auto-play on navigate leaves such a book waiting. A book that ends and stops there, rather than starting again or continuing to the next book, rests finished at its end while its window stays open: Now Playing shows it paused at its end, so the AirPods and the media keys still reach TextMouth, and Previous Track or Skip Backward, from them or from the reader's own Skip Backward, moves back by the skip unit from the end and plays from there, so you can hear its last words again, with the skip's sound and then Play's. It plays again only while no other book plays: with another book playing, Skip Backward in the finished book's window moves its cursor back without playing, leaves the other book playing, and the book is finished no longer. When a sleep timer set for the end of the heading or of the book stops, at its end, a book that would continue to the next book, the timer is spent and the book rests finished: Skip Backward plays its end again, at full volume, and when it ends this time, the next book starts. Skip Forward or Next Track by a number of seconds stays at the end and plays nothing, while a forward skip by sentence, paragraph, heading, or bookmark that finds a later place moves there without playing. That move, or any other jump, such as to a heading, ends the finished state, as Release Media Controls does: the book is then an ordinary stopped book, whose skips move the cursor without playing, and Play plays from the cursor, or, while the cursor is still at the end, does what Play does at a book's end (see When a book ends, below). Closing the window, or quitting TextMouth, keeps it: the book is saved at its end, and opens finished again. A book that continues to the next book, in its folder or in the reading list, doesn't rest finished, though it too is saved at its end: as VoiceOver says "Starting: Dune", Now Playing shows it paused at its end until Dune plays, and a rewind pressed in that moment moves its cursor back without playing over the book announced. Play pressed in that moment, from the AirPods, a media key, or the keyboard, starts Dune at once instead of half a second later, and until Dune starts, Play never plays the finished book: once Dune has been asked for, Play or Play/Pause reaching the finished book says "Still starting: Dune"; once Dune plays, Play in the finished book's window plays it as usual. Release Media Controls is in the Playback menu while the window's book holds the media controls, meaning Now Playing shows it, playing or paused: a finished book counts, and so does a book whose speech stopped where the speech engine refused the text or where the sleep timer stopped it. It hands the controls back to the system and says "Media controls released". Once another book plays, that book holds them, and the first book's window no longer offers Release Media Controls; a key you gave it then changes nothing there and says "Media controls not released: this book does not hold them." Closing a book's window, or the book being stopped, as when a chapter can't be downloaded or its text is replaced, takes its book out of Now Playing whenever Now Playing shows it, even from behind another book's window; the book whose window you used last is shown instead if it was played and is paused, and otherwise the AirPods and the media keys go back to other apps. A Pause that pauses nothing, as one reaching a book already paused or finished, makes no pause sound, and one reaching a book that doesn't hold the media controls, such as one that never played, was stopped, or released them, tells the system nothing, so the AirPods stay with the app or the book they are with. Saving a book's metadata, in its window or the Library window, or any other change of its title or author, such as the metadata refresh after an update, shows its new title in Now Playing only while the book holds the media controls, a finished book included, so a save never takes the AirPods from the app or the book you were listening to.
- The sleep timer runs for a set number of minutes, a preset or typed, or stops at a place in the book: its sheet's Stop At section sets one that stops at the end of the heading, or at the end of the book, for text books and audiobooks alike. Headings are one concept for any book: where the next heading of any level begins, as Next Heading finds it, or the book ends, in a text book of any format or an audiobook, whose chapters, tracks, and chapter markers are its headings. End of heading pauses playback where the heading playing ends: within a chapter, where the next heading begins, with the cursor put back at that heading for Play to start it; at a chapter's end, when the next chapter opens with the next heading, as every audiobook chapter does, with the reader waiting at the start of that chapter, which Play starts; and at the book's end, which ends the last heading. A timer for the end of the heading set where there is nothing to read, as on a book's cover, waits for the end of the heading that playback starts in, so Play does not stop in silence at once. A next chapter that has text before its first heading, or no heading at all, goes on under the same heading, so playback goes on into it. End of heading took the place of End of chapter on October 6, 2026, and Restart Sleep Timer after a timer saved as End of chapter, or as End of document, arms End of heading. VoiceOver says "Sleep timer: stops at the end of this heading." as the timer is set, reads its status the same way in the sheet and in the reader, and says "Sleep timer expired at the end of the heading. Playback paused." where it stops; stopped at the book's end, the reader says instead that the book finished and why nothing follows, as in "Finished: Moby-Dick. Next book not started because of the sleep timer." End of book pauses playback when the book ends, before the book starts again or the next book starts. An audiobook fades out toward a stop at a chapter's end over the sleep timer's Fade out time, in Playback settings, when one is set; speech is not faded toward such a stop, and an m4b's chapter marker inside one file stops the timer a moment past the marker, without a fade, the cursor put back at the marker. When a timer counting minutes runs out and a Fade out time is set, playback fades out over that time before it pauses: an audiobook in its own audio, speech playing from the audio TextMouth prepared in that audio, and speech read straight from the voice in ever quieter stretches, the voice starting again from the word it had reached. Since October 10, 2026, speech read straight from the voice no longer moves to the prepared audio as the fade begins, a move that could repeat or skip a few words. Each reader window has a sleep timer of its own, which belongs to the window and pauses only its book: setting, restarting, or cancelling a timer in one window leaves every other window's timer as it is, and another window's playback, even of the same book, never pauses or ends it. A book you open in a second window while the first has a timer, running or paused, starts with no timer, and playing it neither runs nor stops the first window's. A window keeps its timer when the book it shows changes in place, as when listening goes on to the next book in the same window (see When a book ends, below); a new window that listening goes on to takes the timer once its book has loaded; closing a window stops only its own timer. Each window's Sleep Timer sheet shows, sets, restarts, and cancels that window's timer, and the sheets of two windows can be open at once. Show Sleep Timer and Restart Sleep Timer from the menu or the keyboard act in the window in front. Restart Sleep Timer from the AirPods or the media gesture acts on the book you are hearing: the window of the book that is playing or, while none plays, of the book whose window you used last. A restart in a window with no timer running starts a timer there, with the duration last started in any window, and VoiceOver says "Timer restarted: 10 minutes."; with a timer running it says "Timer reset." as before. In the sheet, the custom timer's field and its Adjust minutes stepper both say their minutes with the unit, as in "30 minutes", and the stepper moves by 5; a custom timer of one minute is said in the singular, as in "Set timer for 1 minute and start playing".
- When a book ends, TextMouth stops, starts the book again, or continues to the next book in the folder, as Playback settings say under When book ends: Stop, the default; Stop, and Play Starts Over; Loop to Start; or Continue to Next Book. Four settings can decide, the first that applies winning:
  - First, a book read from the reading list follows the list instead (below).
  - Second, each book can override that choice in its own settings (above). The settings sheet's picker, When this book ends, which VoiceOver calls "End of book behavior for this book", names the choice in force, and names Continue to Next Book whether the book's feed or folder plays the book below or the one above. Use Global Default, which VoiceOver calls "Reset to global end-of-book setting" and which shows only while the book has a choice of its own, removes the book's own choice, so that the choice of its feed or folder applies, or the global setting where it has none.
  - Third, a feed and a plain folder each choose for their own books with a pop-up button, "When an episode ends" in Feed Info and "When a book ends" in Folder Settings (see [Podcasts and article feeds](feeds.md) and [The Library window](library-window.md)). Its choices are Play the book below, which is Continue to Next Book; Play the book above, which is Continue to Next Book going up the list or folder instead of down; Stop; and Use the global setting, which leaves the choice to the Playback tab. A feed says episode in place of book, or article or item when its items are those. A folder's choice applies to the books directly in it, never to those in the folders inside it, and the top of the library has none.
  - Last, the global setting, When book ends in the Playback tab, decides.
  - Skip finished, which a folder or a feed keeps for itself (Skip finished books in a folder, Skip finished episodes in a feed), applies whichever setting decides, and is checked unless it was cleared there; a book read from the reading list follows its list's own Skip finished books instead. Continue to Next Book, and a choice to play the book below or above, pass over the books that rest at their end, finished, and go on with the first beyond them that is not; with Skip finished cleared, they go on with the very next book, and a finished book they go on to plays from its beginning, after "Starting: <title>" as for any book. When only finished books lie that way, the book says so, as in "Finished: Dune. Every book after it in the folder is finished.", or, going up, "Finished: Dune. Every book before it in the folder is finished."; and with none that way, "Finished: Dune. No more books in folder." A feed's episodes and articles say the same words.
  - For a feed's episode or article, the next one is the next one its feed lists that way, newest first, downloaded or not (see Listening on to the next episode, in [Podcasts and article feeds](feeds.md)). One that is not on this Mac is downloaded first, and that download goes first in the queue. VoiceOver says "Finished: Ep. 368. Downloading the next episode: Ep. 367." ("the next article" for an article), and "Starting: Ep. 367" when the file arrives. Until it arrives, the episode rests finished at its end, as a book that stops does (above). If the download fails, VoiceOver says "The next episode could not be downloaded, so playback stopped." followed by the download's own sentence, as the episode's row says it; if TextMouth will not start the download, as while it quits, that sentence is "TextMouth is not downloading right now." If you close the window before the file arrives, nothing starts when it does, and a sleep timer the window owns stops, as closing the window a timer belongs to stops it. Nothing starts either, and nothing is said of the download, if you have moved on in another way: played the episode again, or taken the media controls for another book, by playing it or by bringing its window to the front. - A book's text can hold what looks like markup, such as the <sm> and </sm> one novel marks its silent messages with, and the speech engine of macOS 26.3.1 takes such tags for speech markup: it skipped the rest of a passage after an opening tag, and refused a passage holding a closing tag alone, after which the book's speech stopped and couldn't be started again. Since October 7, 2026, TextMouth hands the voice each tag with its two angle brackets changed to single angle quotation marks, which the engine doesn't take for markup, so the brackets are silent and what is between them, a tag's name included, is spoken. The engine takes for an opening tag any name of letters, figures, or symbols between angle brackets, with no space or slash inside it and spaces around it or not, such as <sm>, < sm >, <5>, <...>, <=>, or <*>; such a name followed by name=value attributes, as in <a href="x">; and empty brackets, <> or < >. Each of these made it skip the rest of a passage without a sound, and TextMouth silences the brackets of every one, and of every closing tag, such as </sm>. A less-than or greater-than sign that isn't part of a tag, as in "x < y and y > z", is still read aloud, except in audio TextMouth prepared again with every angle bracket silent (below), an ampersand is left for the voice, which reads it as "and", and the text on screen and on a braille display is unchanged. A null character (U+0000), which a badly converted file can hold, made the speech engine drop the rest of a passage without a sound, and without a refusal TextMouth could hear; since October 10, 2026 TextMouth hands the voice a space in its place. No other control character dropped anything. Where Am I?, which can quote the book, and Speak Info, which speaks titles, are spoken the same way. Should the speech engine still refuse a passage of a text book, TextMouth starts the voice afresh at once, since the one that refused would never speak again. If TextMouth has finished preparing the chapter's audio in the background, as it does while it speaks any chapter of up to 100,000 characters, speech goes on from that audio at the refused passage, from the last word heard in it or from its start, so nothing heard is heard twice. If the speech engine refuses the chapter's text as TextMouth prepares its audio in the background, TextMouth prepares the audio once more with every angle bracket silent, so that a refusal while the voice reads has that audio to go on from; speech played from that audio says no less-than or greater-than sign. Without prepared audio to go on from, TextMouth tries the passage once more from there, with every angle bracket in the rest of the passage silent, so a less-than sign there isn't read; the passages after it are read as before. That one more try belongs to the place: once speech has gone on from it, a refusal met at that same place later, as after a jump back to it, is tried once more too. Refused there again, with no prepared audio to go on from, speech stops with the cursor at that passage, and the book's window shows a sheet headed "Speech Stopped", with one sentence under the heading, which VoiceOver reads with it: "The Mac's speech engine refused to speak the text here, twice, so speech stopped. Play tries again from the same place." Below them, a read-only text area labelled Details, which VoiceOver reads line by line and a braille display pans, holds a report for finding the cause: the book and its format; the place, as the brief Where Am I? says it, then the chapter, and the character where speech stopped, counted both in the book's text and in the spoken text, whose offsets the speech log gives; how many times that place was refused while the window has been open; the time, as the logs write it; up to 100 characters before where speech stopped and 400 from it, of the book's text and of the text exactly as it was handed to the speech engine, after the substitutions and the markup guard, with where the refused text begins and ends said in words on lines of their own; the markup guard in force; the voice, the speed, and the rate, pitch, and volume handed to the engine; macOS's version; TextMouth's build; and the last 40 lines the book's speech logged, including the routine lines that the speech log's file keeps only while tracing is on, which hold offsets and lengths, never the book's words.
