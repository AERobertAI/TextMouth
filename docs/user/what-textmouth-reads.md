# What TextMouth reads

Part of [TextMouth's documentation](../../README.md).

- Text books, spoken with any system voice:
  - EPUB files.
  - Word documents (.docx).
  - DAISY books, versions 2.02 and 3: a book's folder, its navigation or package file (ncc.html or a .opf file), or a ZIP holding the book.
  - PDF files. A page that holds only a scanned image is read with the Mac's own text recognition.
  - HTML, Markdown, and plain text files, whatever their text encoding and line endings.
  - Text you type or paste with New from Text.
  - In EPUB, HTML, Word, and Markdown books, a table is read a row at a time: each row is a paragraph of its own, with a pause after it, and the cells in a row are separate words.
- Audiobooks, played at the speed you choose:
  - Single files in MP3, M4A, M4B, WAV, AIFF, FLAC, ALAC, AAC, or Ogg format (.ogg, .oga, or .opus, holding Vorbis, Opus, or FLAC audio), and video files in MPEG-4 (.mp4), M4V, or QuickTime (.mov) format, whose sound alone plays. Ogg audio plays on macOS 15.4 and later; on an earlier macOS its import says the file can't be opened as audio.
  - A ZIP of audio tracks, which becomes one book with a heading for each track. A manifest.json in the ZIP, at its top level or inside the one folder that holds everything, as Finder's Compress command makes it, can name the book and its author, put the tracks in your order with titles of their own, group them under headings, and set the pauses between them. An import whose manifest has a mistake stops and says what is wrong and where (see [The audiobook manifest](audiobook-manifest.md)).
  - A DAISY audio book in a ZIP, which becomes one audiobook whose sections are its headings. A section plays all of its heading's narration, even where it runs on into other audio files. A DAISY audiobook imported by an earlier version keeps one stretch of audio per section until it is imported again.
- Transcripts of audiobooks, on macOS 26 and later:
  - Transcribe Audio… transcribes an audiobook with the Mac's own speech recognizer (SpeechAnalyzer), which asks no permission and sends nothing anywhere: the book's audio never leaves this Mac (see [Transcribing an audiobook](transcribing.md)). The transcript becomes the book's text: the reader shows it around the sentence being narrated, following the narration as the book plays, while positions and bookmarks go on counting time. Where Am I? ends with the sentence being heard, the one the text is shown around, quoting up to 40 words of it. Edit Text opens that text in your own editor (see [Editing a book's text](editing-book-text.md)).
  - Transcripts are in English unless you choose another language in Settings, under Playback, Transcription, or with transcriptionLocale in config.json (see [Editing settings in config.json](config-json.md)): English as this Mac's region writes it when the Mac is set to English, such as en-GB in the United Kingdom, and otherwise US English. Each language needs its speech model, which macOS downloads from Apple only when you ask for it in the Transcribe Audio sheet, and keeps for other apps too.
  - Clean Up Transcript (Experimental)… corrects, by rules you write, words the recognizer always writes another way, such as a name in a series of books, and the last cleanup can be undone (see [Transcribing an audiobook](transcribing.md)). TextMouth comes with no rules of its own: measured on 38,834 words read aloud, no correction was right in every book. TRANSCRIPT-CLEANUP-RESEARCH-2026-10-04.md, in the same folder as this file, says which words the recognizer gets wrong, and what a cleanup can fix.
  - TextMouth itself still runs on macOS 14 and 15, which can't transcribe: there Transcribe Audio… only says that transcription needs macOS 26 or later.
- Podcasts and article feeds, in RSS 2.0, RSS 1.0, Atom, or JSON Feed (1.0 and 1.1), with Apple's podcast elements and Podcasting 2.0's:
  - TextMouth can subscribe to a feed by its address, by a feed:, pcast:, or itpc: link, by a web page that names its feed, or by searching Apple's podcast directory, and lists the feed's episodes and articles in a library folder of its own, named after it (see [Podcasts and article feeds](feeds.md)). An episode downloads as an audiobook, a video episode as an audiobook that plays its sound, and an article as an HTML book, read from its web page when the feed gives only a short summary that links one, though an article TextMouth downloads by itself reads its page only on a network without data limits, and otherwise holds the feed's own text; each download is kept in the feed's own folder in the data directory, apart from the library's books (see Feeds, under [Where TextMouth keeps your library](where-your-library-lives.md)).
  - Feeds refresh when TextMouth opens and every hour while it runs, unless the Feeds tab of Settings says otherwise, skipping an hour in Low Power Mode. A feed you unsubscribed from is never refreshed, at launch, every hour, or by Refresh All Feeds, until you choose Resubscribe (see Commands, under [Podcasts and article feeds](feeds.md)). Each feed's List and download choice decides which of its episodes and articles are listed and which download by themselves: unless you choose otherwise, every one the feed lists is listed, and those of the newest 5 not yet played are kept downloaded (see Listing and downloading, under [Podcasts and article feeds](feeds.md)). Those refreshes, and the episodes TextMouth downloads by itself, never use a network with data limits, such as Low Data Mode or a phone's hotspot: such an episode waits, its row saying "Waiting to Download, it downloads once this Mac is on a network without data limits", and is asked for again after the next refresh TextMouth makes by itself on a network without them. A finished episode or article counts among Books Finished, and its download's file is deleted for good a day after it finished, or as the Feeds tab of Settings says, unless it is open in a reader, being transcribed, or being exported, when it goes at a later pass; its place and bookmarks are kept should you download it again (see Commands, under [Podcasts and article feeds](feeds.md)).
  - The feeds, and a search of Apple's podcast directory, are the only things TextMouth itself reaches the network for, and its connections keep no cookies and cache nothing on disk; a feed or episode at a plain http address loads too, since many still use one. A transcription language's speech model is downloaded by macOS, and only when you ask for it (see [Transcribing an audiobook](transcribing.md)).
- TextMouth packages (.tmb folders) from another TextMouth library. A package keeps its reading position, bookmarks, and per-book settings.
