# What TextMouth reads

Part of [TextMouth's documentation](../../README.md).

- Text books, spoken with any system voice:
  - EPUB files. A member the book names in another letter case than its ZIP spells it, as a badly made book may, is found all the same: the exact spelling is looked for first.
  - Word documents (.docx), and Word templates and macro documents (.dotx, .docm, .dotm), which are read the same way and listed as Word documents. No part of a macro project is read, and no macro ever runs.
  - Documents that Apple's textutil tool reads: rich text (.rtf), rich text with attachments (.rtfd), Word 97-2003 documents and templates (.doc, .dot), OpenDocument text and its templates (.odt, .ott), and Word 2003 XML (.xml, below). Each becomes a text book like any other, so it reopens, searches, and keeps its place, bookmarks, and skipped headings. An .rtfd is a package that the Finder shows as one file, and it imports as one book.
    - textutil runs in a separate process that TextMouth watches, never inside TextMouth, so a hang or a crash in Apple's readers costs that process and never TextMouth or the import. It loads nothing that the document links to and fetches nothing from the network. It runs once for each import, and the book's title and author come from that same run. Cancel in the import overlay stops it at once (see [Adding books](adding-books.md)). [Where TextMouth keeps your library](where-your-library-lives.md) gives the limits of time, memory, and processor time at which TextMouth stops it, where it writes, and what stops one that a crash left behind.
    - Headings are found from font sizes and bold, since textutil writes every heading as an ordinary paragraph. The body size is the commonest size, counted by characters. A paragraph of at most 120 characters that is larger than the body is a heading, and so is one that is bold throughout at the body's size, unless more than half of the body is bold. A heading's level comes from its size's rank, the largest size being level 1, in at most six levels, and a heading found from bold follows the larger sizes. Text in a table or a list is never a heading.
    - Bold and italic text keeps its emphasis, as in the other text formats, and the marks that show it are never spoken. Pictures are silent. The instruction of a Word field, such as a table of contents or a link, is never read; the field's visible result is.
    - The content decides, not the extension. A file whose content is rich text is read as rich text whatever its name, and a .doc that is really a web page or Word 2003 XML is read as what it is, the web page by TextMouth's own HTML reader.
  - DAISY books, versions 2.02 and 3: a book's folder, its navigation or package file (ncc.html or a .opf file), or a ZIP holding the book. A DTBook file on its own (.xml) imports as a book too, and so does a NIMAS package, which is a DTBook with its package file.
  - XML files (.xml): TextMouth reads the start of each one and names its kind by the element it begins with. A DTBook goes to the DAISY reader, Word 2003 XML goes to textutil, a PEF braille file goes to the braille reader (below), and any other XML is refused by name.
  - Braille files: BRF files (.brf), BRL files (.brl), and PEF files (.pef). TextMouth translates them to print with liblouis, using the braille code chosen in the Braille tab of Settings (see [Settings](settings.md)), and the print becomes a text book like any other, so it reopens, searches, and keeps its place, bookmarks, and skipped headings. A book keeps the code it was imported with, through Remove from Library Only and adding it back too, so changing the setting never changes what an imported book says. A book read with one of this Mac's tables (see [Settings](settings.md)) also keeps the liblouis version of this Mac's tables, and if a macOS update changes them, TextMouth refuses to rebuild that book's text and keeps the text as it was, with this sentence: "This Mac's braille table “mac:fr-bfu-g2.ctb” has changed since the book was read with it, from liblouis 3.34.0 to 3.35.0, so TextMouth keeps the book's text as it was." When this Mac's tables name no version now, it says the table "may have changed since the book was read with liblouis 3.34.0" instead. A book that recorded no version is never refused for this. Book Info and Speak Info name the code (see [The Library window](library-window.md)).
    - A BRF or BRL file holds braille ASCII, in upper or lower case, with each line ended by CR LF, LF, or CR. A tab counts as one blank cell. A BRF ends each page with a form feed, and a BRF with none is cut into pages of 25 lines. A BRL has no layout, so each of its lines that is not blank is a paragraph and it has no headings. A PEF file must be six-dot braille: eight-dot PEF is refused.
    - Headings, paragraphs, and list items are found from the layout, as BANA's Braille Formats (2016) lays it out. A centered line, or a centered run of lines, is a level-1 heading; a line at cell 5 is a level-2 heading, and a line at cell 7 is level 3. Paragraphs and list items are found from their indents, and each list item is read as a paragraph. Page numbers are left out of the text, so they are never spoken.
    - Emphasis: reading Unified English Braille keeps what its emphasis signs say. Italic and bold come into the book's text as italic and bold; underline, script, and the typefaces a transcriber defines come in as italic, the nearest thing the text holds. As in the other text formats, the marks that show emphasis are never spoken. English Braille, American Edition, and most of this Mac's tables are read as plain braille, since liblouis cannot read their emphasis back exactly; TextMouth's braille helper tests each table for this when the table is used, so none is picked out by name. Contracted (Grade 2) UEB has one fault, liblouis's own: just before the end of an emphasized passage it can read a whole-word contraction as the letter or sign its cell also is, so that "people" comes back as "p" and "his" as a double quotation mark. Uncontracted UEB reads those cases exactly. A braille book imported before emphasis was kept keeps its text as it was until its text is read again, as Redo Text Extraction reads it; the new reading adds the emphasis, changes no word, and moves no saved place. Export writes a book's emphasis into braille too (see [Exporting books](exporting-books.md)).
    - A PEF names its book's title and author in its head, and the import reads them from the first 64 KB of the file. A BRF or BRL names neither, so its book is titled by its file's name without the extension, and its author is Unknown.
    - A braille file of more than 32 MB is refused, and so is one of more than 100,000 pages or 4,000,000 lines (see [Adding books](adding-books.md) for the words of each refusal).
    - Not read yet: running heads, Roman-numeral page numbers, and words divided at a line's end. A list laid out by another tool may read as plain paragraphs.
    - liblouis runs in a separate process that TextMouth watches, never inside TextMouth, so a hang or a crash in liblouis costs that process and never TextMouth or the import. [Where TextMouth keeps your library](where-your-library-lives.md) gives its limits.
  - PDF files. A page that holds only a scanned image is read with the Mac's own text recognition.
  - HTML, Markdown, and plain text files, whatever their text encoding and line endings.
  - Safari web archives (.webarchive). Only the archive's main page is read, as an HTML file is read (or a text file, for a text page), so its headings, lists, and tables read as in an HTML book. The archive's pictures, scripts, style sheets, and frames, and every address it names, are never read, decoded, or fetched, and WebKit is not used.
  - SSML files (.ssml), read as text: the markup is removed, and the paragraphs and sentences are kept.
    - A sub element's alias is read in place of its written text: <sub alias="World Wide Web Consortium">W3C</sub> reads "World Wide Web Consortium".
    - The text of desc, meta, metadata, and lexicon elements is left out. An audio element's sound file is never read or fetched, and only the text inside the element is read.
    - A break, a change of voice, or a sentence's start keeps the words on either side of it apart.
    - The file is never given to the speech engine as SSML, which would ignore the speed you set. Its words are spoken in your voice at your speed, whatever voice, speed, or pitch the markup asks for.
  - Text you type or paste with New from Text.
  - Pages, which Next Page, Previous Page, and Go to Page… move by (see [Reader windows](reader-windows.md)), are numbered as each format numbers them. A book already in your library gains pages when its text is read again (see [Where TextMouth keeps your library](where-your-library-lives.md)):
    - EPUB: the book's page list or, when it has none, its page-break marks. A mark's own text is not read aloud when it is no more than its page's label, the print page's number and perhaps a caption of up to 32 characters; any longer or other text in a mark is read, after its page marker, and a mark with no aria-label or title that holds more than that begins no page.
    - HTML files, and Safari web archives as HTML files are read: page-break marks.
    - PDF files: every page, with the page labels the PDF gives, such as i, ii, 1, 2, and A-1, or its number when it gives none. A page with no text is still a page.
    - Word documents, templates, and macro documents: the page breaks the author wrote, paragraphs set to start a page, sections that start a page, and the page breaks Word recorded when it last laid the document out. Pages are numbered from 1; a section can restart the number and set its format (decimal, lower or upper case roman numerals, or lower or upper case letters; any other format counts as decimal). A document Word never laid out, such as one another program made, holds only its author's page breaks, so TextMouth shows fewer pages than Word would.
    - DAISY books: the page numbers the book gives, which are no longer read aloud as "[Page 12]". A DAISY 2.02 page entry's own number or caption, as "12" under the label "Page 12", is not read either; any longer or other text it points at is.
    - Rich text, rich text with attachments, Word 97-2003, OpenDocument text, and Word 2003 XML: the page breaks textutil reports in a second run, numbered from 1, with the words textutil ran together at a break kept apart. A .doc holds no layout of Word's own, so it shows fewer pages than Word would. textutil keeps a break written `\page` in rich text and loses one written `\pagebb` or `\sect`, and it loses an .odt's page breaks. A break whose surroundings differ in textutil's two readings is dropped, never placed wrongly; a Word field such as a table of contents or a link no longer puts the two readings out of step, since the breaks are placed before textutil's field instructions are removed, so a .doc holding one shows every page, with the words kept apart at each break.
    - BRF and PEF braille files: every braille page, the pages an embosser or a braille display counts, each named by its braille page number, such as 12 or p1, or by its place among the file's pages when it has none. A page that begins in the middle of a paragraph begins at its first word there. A BRL file has no pages.
    - Plain text: each form feed between the words begins a page, numbered from 1, at the first line after it that is read; a form feed before the first words or after the last begins none, so a text without one between words has no pages.
    - SSML files have no pages. A Markdown file has pages only where it holds page lines (see [Editing a book's text](editing-book-text.md)).
  - In EPUB, HTML, Word, and Markdown books, a table is read a row at a time: each row is a paragraph of its own, with a pause after it, and the cells in a row are separate words.
  - A rich text, Word 97-2003, OpenDocument, or Word 2003 XML document (for an .rtfd, all the files in it), a web archive, an SSML file, or a DTBook on its own that holds more than 64 MB is refused, since that is the most TextMouth reads from one document.
  - In the Library window, a book's row names its format: "Rich Text" for an .rtf, "Rich Text with Attachments" for an .rtfd, "Word 97-2003 Document" for a .doc or .dot, "OpenDocument Text" for an .odt or .ott, "Word 2003 XML", "Web Archive", "SSML", or "Braille" for a BRF, BRL, or PEF. A Word template or macro document says "Word Document", and a DTBook says "DAISY".
- Audiobooks, played at the speed you choose:
  - Single files in MP3, M4A, M4B, WAV (RF64 and BWF too), AIFF, FLAC, ALAC, or AAC format, and video files in MPEG-4 (.mp4), M4V, or QuickTime (.mov) format, whose sound alone plays. TextMouth plays these as they are, except an MPEG-4 or QuickTime file whose sound is not AAC, Apple Lossless, or MP3, which it converts like the audio below.
  - Audio that TextMouth converts as it is imported, since macOS can decode it but TextMouth does not play it as it is: Ogg (.ogg, .oga, or .opus, holding Vorbis, Opus, or FLAC audio), AC-3 (.ac3), E-AC-3 (.ec3, .eac3), MP1 and MP2 (.mp1, .mp2, .mpa), AMR (.amr, .awb), 3GPP audio (.3gp, .3g2, .3ga), NeXT/Sun audio (.au, .snd), AIFC (.aifc), CAF (.caf), and Wave64 (.w64). Each file becomes an .m4a file in the book's package, and only that file is played. A file is converted wherever it comes from: a single file, a ZIP, a folder, a playlist, or a feed's episode. The book's package keeps only the converted file, unless Keep originals of converted audio is on in the Storage tab of Settings (see [Settings](settings.md)).
    - What a file holds decides, not its extension. An Ogg file named .m4a, or an MP2 feed episode saved as .mp3, is converted all the same.
    - Lossy audio becomes AAC at about its own bit rate: it stays near its size, but a second lossy encoding can lose a little. The rate is the next standard AAC rate at or above the source's average (64 to 192 kbit/s for stereo, half that for mono). Lossless audio that TextMouth converts (PCM in CAF, AIFC, AU, or Wave64; Apple Lossless or FLAC in CAF; Ogg FLAC) becomes Apple Lossless at its own bit depth (16, 20, 24, or 32 bits) and loses nothing. Plain WAV, AIFF, and FLAC files are not converted at all.
    - TextMouth writes at most two channels, so audio of more than two channels is mixed to stereo as it is converted, with Apple's standard mix: the centre channel, where speech usually is, keeps its place in both ears. Such a book plays about 11 to 14 dB quieter than a stereo book: 11 when the source is linear PCM and 14 when it is AC-3. A file of more than two channels that does not say which is which can't be mixed, and is refused.
    - Ogg: TextMouth converts Ogg audio on macOS 15.4 and later, which can read it; it never plays Ogg itself, since macOS's Vorbis decoder can lock up while playing (October 14, 2026). It does not ask macOS's audio to open an Ogg file for anything else either: it reads the file's title, artist, album, genre, year, and length from the file's own bytes with code of its own, and only afconvert, run as a separate process that TextMouth watches, decodes it. An Ogg file that claims to last more than a year is refused as unreadable. A file cut from a longer stream, as a live broadcast joined part way through is, reports the length it holds, not the length counted from the start of the broadcast, and a file that carries an Ogg Skeleton track beside its audio is read for its audio and imports like any other.
    - Every other converted format is read by Apple's afinfo tool, which says what the file holds, and then converted by afconvert, each run as a separate process that TextMouth watches (see [Where TextMouth keeps your library](where-your-library-lives.md)). A file of one of them that claims to last more than a year, or a length that its size cannot hold, is refused as unreadable. A Mac that can't read or convert a format refuses a file of it by name when it is imported, and nothing is added.
  - An M4B, M4A, MP4, M4V, or MOV file whose only chapter list is a Nero list (some audiobook tools write only that) has its chapters as headings, an untitled one called "Chapter" and its number.
  - An M3U or M3U8 playlist (.m3u, .m3u8), which becomes one audiobook of the files it lists, a heading for each, in the playlist's order (see [Adding books](adding-books.md)).
  - A ZIP of audio tracks, which becomes one book with a heading for each track. A manifest.json in the ZIP, at its top level or inside the one folder that holds everything, as Finder's Compress command makes it, can name the book and its author, put the tracks in your order with titles of their own, group them under headings, and set the pauses between them. An import whose manifest has a mistake stops and says what is wrong and where (see [The audiobook manifest](audiobook-manifest.md)).
  - A DAISY audio book in a ZIP, which becomes one audiobook whose sections are its headings. A section plays all of its heading's narration, even where it runs on into other audio files. A DAISY audiobook imported by an earlier version keeps one stretch of audio per section until it is imported again.
- Transcripts of audiobooks, on macOS 26 and later:
  - Transcribe Audio… transcribes an audiobook with the Mac's own speech recognizer (SpeechAnalyzer), which asks no permission and sends nothing anywhere: the book's audio never leaves this Mac (see [Transcribing an audiobook](transcribing.md)). The transcript becomes the book's text: the reader shows it around the sentence being narrated, following the narration as the book plays, while positions and bookmarks go on counting time. Where Am I? ends with the sentence being heard, the one the text is shown around, quoting up to 40 words of it. Edit Text opens that text in your own editor (see [Editing a book's text](editing-book-text.md)).
  - Transcripts are in English unless you choose another language in Settings, under Playback, Transcription, or with transcriptionLocale in config.json (see [Editing settings in config.json](config-json.md)): English as this Mac's region writes it when the Mac is set to English, such as en-GB in the United Kingdom, and otherwise US English. Each language needs its speech model, which macOS downloads from Apple only when you ask for it in the Transcribe Audio sheet, and keeps for other apps too.
  - Clean Up Transcript (Experimental)… corrects, by rules you write, words the recognizer always writes another way, such as a name in a series of books, and the last cleanup can be undone (see [Transcribing an audiobook](transcribing.md)). TextMouth comes with no rules of its own: measured on 38,834 words read aloud, no correction was right in every book. TRANSCRIPT-CLEANUP-RESEARCH-2026-10-04.md, in the same folder as this file, says which words the recognizer gets wrong, and what a cleanup can fix.
  - TextMouth itself still runs on macOS 14 and 15, which can't transcribe: there Transcribe Audio… only says that transcription needs macOS 26 or later.
- Podcasts and article feeds, in RSS 2.0, RSS 1.0, Atom, or JSON Feed (1.0 and 1.1), with Apple's podcast elements and Podcasting 2.0's:
  - TextMouth can subscribe to a feed by its address, by a feed:, pcast:, or itpc: link, by a web page that names its feed, or by searching Apple's podcast directory, and lists the feed's episodes and articles in a library folder of its own, named after it (see [Podcasts and article feeds](feeds.md)). An episode downloads as an audiobook, a video episode as an audiobook that plays its sound, and an article as an HTML book, read from its web page when the feed gives only a short summary that links one, though an article TextMouth downloads by itself reads its page only on a network without data limits, and otherwise holds the feed's own text; each download is kept in the feed's own folder in the data directory, apart from the library's books (see Feeds, under [Where TextMouth keeps your library](where-your-library-lives.md)).
  - Feeds refresh when TextMouth opens and every hour while it runs, unless the Feeds tab of Settings says otherwise, skipping an hour in Low Power Mode. A feed you unsubscribed from is never refreshed, at launch, every hour, or by Refresh All Feeds, until you choose Resubscribe (see Commands, under [Podcasts and article feeds](feeds.md)). Each feed's List and download choice decides which of its episodes and articles are listed and which download by themselves: unless you choose otherwise, every one the feed lists is listed, and those of the newest 5 not yet played are kept downloaded (see Listing and downloading, under [Podcasts and article feeds](feeds.md)). Those refreshes, and the episodes TextMouth downloads by itself, never use a network with data limits, such as Low Data Mode or a phone's hotspot: such an episode waits, its row saying "Waiting to Download, it downloads once this Mac is on a network without data limits", and is asked for again after the next refresh TextMouth makes by itself on a network without them. A finished episode or article counts among Books Finished, and its download's file is deleted for good a day after it finished, or as the Feeds tab of Settings says, unless it is open in a reader, being transcribed, or being exported, when it goes at a later pass; its place and bookmarks are kept should you download it again (see Commands, under [Podcasts and article feeds](feeds.md)).
  - The feeds, and a search of Apple's podcast directory, are the only things TextMouth itself reaches the network for, and its connections keep no cookies and cache nothing on disk; a feed or episode at a plain http address loads too, since many still use one. A transcription language's speech model is downloaded by macOS, and only when you ask for it (see [Transcribing an audiobook](transcribing.md)).
- TextMouth packages (.tmb folders) from another TextMouth library. A package keeps its reading position, bookmarks, and per-book settings.
