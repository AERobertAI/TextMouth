# Timers and statistics

Part of [TextMouth's architecture](../../../ARCHITECTURE.md).

- The sleep timer.
  - Owner: SleepTimerService.shared, the app's one timer, and its owner: the readerID of the reader that started it, an identity of each reader window's own (ReaderViewModel.readerID), which also owns the reader's Now Playing session, so two windows of the same book are told apart. What the timer waits for is a SleepTimerDuration: a number of minutes, the end of the heading (endOfHeading, the one place-based timer for any document since October 6, 2026, when End of chapter folded into it: where the next heading of any level begins or the book ends; an audiobook's chapters, tracks, and marker segments each begin with a heading, so its heading ends with its chapter), or the end of the book (endOfBook).
  - Who may write it: a reader starts, cancels, or restarts the timer, passing its readerID, and starting a timer replaces any other. Pausing and resuming the countdown, and the end of the heading an End of heading timer waits for, count only when they come from the owning reader, so another window's playback, even of the same book, never affects the timer. That timer waits for the heading after the cursor when it was set (ReaderViewModel.sleepTimerNextHeading, found once a loading book has its headings; nil after the last heading, which ends with the book): the reader's refresh, at most every 0.12 s, asks whether playback reached it (stopForSleepTimerIfPlaybackReachedHeadingEnd, through waitsForHeadingEnd(ownedBy:)) and, if so, stops playback, puts the cursor at that heading, and ends the timer (expireAtHeadingEnd(by:)); a move forward to or past it ends the timer the same way, after the move's own announcement (endSleepTimerIfMoveReachedHeadingEnd); a move back leaves it waiting. Where a chapter's speech or audio ends, the reader asks whether its own timer stops playback there and, if so, ends it (expireAtChapterEnd(endingTheHeading:endingTheBook:by:), deciding through stopsPlaybackAtChapterEnd(endingTheHeading:endingTheBook:ownedBy:)): End of heading stops there when the next chapter opens with the next heading (ReaderViewModel.chapterEnding(beforeChapter:), through headingHasBegun(_:atStartOfChapter:text:)), which is where End of chapter and End of heading differed, since a next chapter with text before its first heading, or none, plays on under the heading; End of heading and End of book both stop at the last chapter's end, the book's. notifyHeadingBoundary and notifyDocumentEnd are gone. A timer ended so stops as stop() stops it, with no fade left to run and no expiry callback. The last timer started is saved, as sleepTimer.lastUsedDuration, for Restart Sleep Timer, and a saved endOfChapter or endOfDocument restores as End of heading. A reader that closes stops the timer if it owns it, and stops being its last owner (forgetLastOwner(_:)), so Restart Sleep Timer from a media gesture goes to the focused reader instead.
  - How others observe it: every sleep timer sheet shows the shared state, with End of heading and End of book in its Stop At section, after the minute presets and the custom minutes (SleepTimerView.endOfHeadingTitle, endOfHeadingLabel, and endOfHeadingHint, and the End of book constants), and VoiceOver reads the timer's status, in the sheet and in the reader, as spokenLabel, such as "Sleep timer: 4:59 remaining" or "Sleep timer: stops at the end of this heading". The owning reader's callbacks fade out and pause its playback when a timer counting minutes runs out (sleepTimerExpiredAnnouncement): an audiobook through its volume, and speech through setSleepTimerFadeVolume(_:duration:), which never switches direct speech to rendered audio (speech, under media playback, above). A stop at the heading's end, within a chapter or with one, announces sleepTimerStoppedAtHeadingEndAnnouncement, "Sleep timer expired at the end of the heading. Playback paused."; at a chapter's end the reader stops before the next chapter starts (stopForSleepTimerAtChapterEnd(of:nextChapterIndex:)): its cursor waits, saved, at the next chapter's start, and a text book discards the chapter rendered ahead unplayed. At the book's end, handleEndOfBook starts the book again only when no timer of the reader's stops there, and continues to the next book only when no timer of the reader's is in effect at all, since the timer could never pause the next book's reader (sleepTimerEndsListeningHere); VoiceOver hears why nothing follows. An audiobook fades out toward a stop at a chapter's end over the timer's fade-out time, its volume following the time left in the chapter at the playback speed (fadeOutBeforeSleepTimerStop(), from AudioPlaybackService.timeLeftToPlay), so a pause holds the volume where it is and cancelling or replacing the timer restores it; speech is not faded toward such a stop. An m4b's chapter marker inside one file stops the timer through the heading path, a moment past the marker, at the next refresh after an audio position update, without a fade, since AudioPlaybackService.timeLeftToPlay measures the file, not the chapter. textmouth_timer.log records each timer from start to expiry.
  - Files: Services/SleepTimerService.swift, Models/DocumentContent.swift, Views/Reader/SleepTimerView.swift, ViewModels/ReaderViewModel+Playback.swift, ViewModels/ReaderViewModel.swift, Services/AudioPlaybackService.swift.
- Listening checkpoints.
  - Owner: ReadingStatsService.shared: the statistics, saved as reading_stats.json, and the open listening session.
  - Who may write it: a reader starts a session when it plays and ends it when it pauses or stops. Every 60 seconds a timer checkpoints the open session, crediting the time since the previous checkpoint and saving, so a crash loses at most a minute. markBookFinished(bookID:title:) counts each book once (P07): it writes an entry only for a book Books Finished counts for the first time, returns whether it did, and posts bookDidFinish at every end it is told of, so a feed item marked unplayed and heard again is played again; a rewind's replay of a finished end tells it nothing (finishAtTheEnd(replayingAFinishedEnd:), the reader, above). The entry records the book with its kind (FinishedBookEntry.kind, a FinishedBookKind: a text book, which an article a feed downloaded is too, an audiobook, or a podcast episode, an audiobook a feed downloaded), told while the library still holds the book, since a played episode's download is soon deleted: the feed engine installs the resolver on the statistics service (ReadingStatsService.finishedBookKind, answered by FeedCenter.finishedBookKind(of:)), which knows its episodes, an audiobook Move to Library… made of an episode among them, even once its feed's record is gone, by its package record's feed link, read once without downloading (cameFromAFeed(_:)), and reads any other book's format from the library. A kind a later version writes reads as none. No save is written while reading_stats.json is only in the cloud (files several Macs write, above).
  - How others observe it: the Books Finished figures count ReadingStats.countedFinishes, each book once, by the first entry naming it, so the repeats an earlier version wrote at each end of a book stay in the file and count once (booksFinishedAllTime, booksFinishedThisYear(at:), in the year of that first finish), and Recently Finished lists recentlyFinished(limit:), the last ten of them, newest first, its rows keyed by entry (FinishedBookEntry is Identifiable by its book and date). The Stats tab reads every figure at one instant on each tick (ReadingStatsFigures), including the open session's time since its last checkpoint, and the books finished by kind (booksFinishedThisYearByKind(at:) and booksFinishedAllTimeByKind, as FinishedBookCounts), a book finished before kinds were recorded counting by the library's answer while the library holds it, and in no kind after; the two Books Finished tiles show the kinds under the count and speak them with it (ReadingStatsTileWording.books(_:byKind:finished:)).
  - Files: Services/ReadingStatsService.swift, Models/ReadingStatsTile.swift, Views/Settings/ReadingStatsView.swift, Services/Feeds/FeedLifecycle.swift.
- Day keys.
  - Owner: ListeningDays. A day key such as "2026-10-02" names one local day, written in the Gregorian calendar, the POSIX locale, and ASCII digits whatever the user's settings, in the time zone current when the time is credited. Time already credited never moves.
  - Who may write it: ReadingStatsService credits time under these keys, and ListeningDayKeyMigration converts, once, keys saved under the system's own calendar (ReadingStats.dayKeyVersion).
  - Files: Services/ListeningDays.swift, Services/ListeningDayKeyMigration.swift, Services/ReadingStatsService.swift.
