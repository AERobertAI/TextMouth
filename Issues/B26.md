## Description

A table's rows are each read as a paragraph whose cells are joined by one space (`MarkdownParser.readTable`, and `HTMLReadingText`, which it matches), so a row such as "Alder Quay 4.2 0.6" cannot be split into its cells by ear or on a braille display: a cell of two words sounds like two cells. This holds for every format whose tables reach content.md (HTML, EPUB, DOCX, ODT, DAISY, and the rest), on both platforms.

Found by officeresearch (research/SLIDES-AND-SHEETS-2026-11-24.md), which proposes ", " between a sheet's cells for the new spreadsheet readers; checked by the orchestrator in MarkdownParser.swift on November 24, 2026.

Open question for the user: how a cell boundary should read in speech and braille for every table: a comma and a space, a semicolon, a short pause in speech with a separator in braille, or the column's header before each cell (as screen readers announce in table navigation). A change of the reading text is a page-reader version bump for the formats whose tables change.
