## Description

**What happens.** `InProcessEngine` times each call with `DispatchTime`, which keeps counting while iOS holds TextMouth suspended and the phone is awake.
- When the listener comes back after a call's limit and its grace, the waiter wakes first and finds the time gone. It abandons the call, though the call was frozen, not stuck.
- Every later call is then refused until TextMouth is closed and opened again, with `InProcessEngineWording`'s sentence, which is untrue here.

**Where.** `TextMouth/Services/InProcessEngine.swift`: `Call.shouldStop` and `wait(for:)`. Braille has run through this engine since 0.5.2 (DR34). FFmpeg's in-app run (FMT54) uses it too.

**Order of events.**
1. A call starts.
2. The listener switches to another app, and iOS suspends TextMouth. An import's background task lasts only about 30 seconds.
3. The listener comes back after more than the call's limit plus its grace.
4. The call is abandoned, and the engine refuses all later work until relaunch.

**Found by** review-phoneffmpeg, November 17, 2026 (finding 1, `build/orchestrator/nov17/review-phoneffmpeg-report.md`).

**The fix,** on `phone/ffmpeg-in-app` with FMT54:
- **Count only the time TextMouth runs,** for each call and for a run's whole limit. Treat the clock's jump across a suspension as time not spent, from a heartbeat's late ticks or the app's lifecycle notices.
- **One judge.** Only the waiter judges a call's time, so a stop check can't fire on the stale clock at resume.
- **A test** with a scripted engine clock, for braille's engine and FFmpeg's.
