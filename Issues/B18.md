## Description

**What happens.** `InProcessEngine` times each call with `DispatchTime`, which keeps counting while iOS holds TextMouth suspended and the phone is awake.
- When the listener comes back after a call's limit and its grace, the waiter wakes first and finds the time gone. It abandons the call, though the call was frozen, not stuck.
- Every later call is then refused until TextMouth is closed and opened again, with `InProcessEngineWording`'s sentence, which is untrue here.

**Where.** `TextMouth/Services/InProcessEngine.swift`: `Call.shouldStop` and `wait(for:)`. Braille has run through this engine since 0.5.2 (DR34). FFmpeg's in-app run (FMT54) uses it too.

**Order of events.**
1. A call starts.
2. The listener switches to another app, and iOS suspends TextMouth. An import's background task lasts only about 30 seconds.
3. The listener comes back after more than the call's limit plus its grace.
4. The call is abandoned, and the engine refuses all later work until relaunch.

**Found by** review-phoneffmpeg, November 17, 2026 (finding 1, `build/orchestrator/nov17/review-phoneffmpeg-report.md`).

**The fix,** on `phone/ffmpeg-in-app` with FMT54:
- **Count only the time TextMouth runs,** for each call and for a run's whole limit. Treat the clock's jump across a suspension as time not spent, from a heartbeat's late ticks or the app's lifecycle notices.
- **One judge.** Only the waiter judges a call's time, so a stop check can't fire on the stale clock at resume.
- **A test** with a scripted engine clock, for braille's engine and FFmpeg's.

## Comments

### 2026-11-17, phoneffmpeg, 12 lines

Fixed on phone/ffmpeg-in-app in 0bd5002e4 (with fd77f7384, a test file's layout); waiting for the orchestrator's merge with FMT54.

- RunningClock (TextMouth/Services/RunningClock.swift) counts only the time TextMouth runs. Each reading counts the uptime since the one before, at most its longest step (1 s). A heartbeat reads the clock every 0.25 s while something holds it, so running time counts whole and only a heartbeat's late tick, a suspension, does not.
- Why a heartbeat and not the app's lifecycle notices: iOS never says it is suspending the app. The notices say only that it went to the background, where TextMouth often keeps running (its audio, an import's background task), so pausing the clock there would count too little, and keeping it running there counts the suspension.
- InProcessEngine takes the clock (RunningClock.app by default), so braille's engine has the same fix. The waiter alone judges a call's time: it looks every quarter second with the clock read anew, tells the work to stop, and abandons the grace later. Call.shouldStop reads no clock, so work that resumes first cannot stop on a stale one.
- A run's whole limit: FFmpeg's in-app run times it by Tool.clock (replacing Tool.uptime), held for the run. Braille's tool times its library by Tool.clock, the app's on the iPhone, so a book's time shared by its batches counts no suspension; on the Mac it is RunningClock.system, the uptime counted whole, so the Mac's measured runs are unchanged.
- Tests, with scripted engine and tool clocks:
  - RunningClockTests;
  - InProcessEngineTests: a call suspended five minutes past its limit is neither told to stop nor abandoned, and shouldStop reads no clock;
  - FFmpegInAppTests: a run suspended between its calls reads to its end;
  - BrailleBatchLimitsTests: a book suspended inside a batch still reads, and the app's tools and engines share RunningClock.app.
- Break proofs (scratch commit 7941f1031, GitHub hand run 44213336710): with every gap counted whole, the clock's gap test, the engine's suspended call (abandoned), the FFmpeg run suspended between calls, and the braille book suspended in a batch all fail on the iPhone (the first two on the Mac too); with shouldStop reading the clock, its test reads it 10,001 times on both; with braille's iPhone tool on the system's uptime, its clock test fails. The heartbeat (a harness in a scratch copy): held 1.2 seconds, a clock with it counted 1.21, the same clock without it 0.50.

### 2026-11-18, phoneffmpeg, 11 lines

The check of the fixes judged RunningClock sound: the cap, the heartbeat ending at rest, the threads, the resume, and no Mac timing changed. Its item 5 is in 272d0bd0b: tests of two holders, of a hold right after a release, and of a hold after the heartbeat stopped.

Break proofs, on the iPhone in runs 44226031638 and 44228522562 and on the Mac in 44228522562:
- When one release ends every hold, the two-holder test fails.
- When a heartbeat that ended at rest stays marked as ticking, the test of a hold after it stopped fails.
- The test of a hold right after a release pins the single lock under which the tick and the hold decide. Only a decision split across two locks would fail it, and then only now and then, so it has no deterministic break.

Train 1117-2249's iPhone crash in BrailleBatchLimitsTests was not the clock.
- Its crash report shows SIGSEGV in liblouis (lou_backTranslate). It was on the thread of a call that FMT54's test for the check's item 4 let be abandoned, beside the next test's own call into liblouis.
- The main thread was in a timed semaphore wait. No RunningClock frame is on any thread, and no thread waits on a lock.
- That test is reverted (1c787ca75).
